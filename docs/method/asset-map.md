# The asset map

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5b. The asset map (`src/classify.py`, `./wbg classify assets`)

Every sentence the search reads (narrative and annex sentences, footnotes, prose table rows; copies once) is asked, for each active asset in `inputs/taxonomy/assets.csv`, the one graded question every stage asks (`classify.graded_question`), with its section and component as context. The grades are the asset scale (`inputs/taxonomy/assets_scale.csv`): 0 not about it, 1 background or another party's work, 2 supported without paying for the asset (technical assistance, policy, studies), 3 financed (built, bought, installed, upgraded, connected or operated). Grades 2 and 3 count together (`read.grades`).

**What is asked: the measures' method.** An asset's question goes to a sentence when the sentence's cosine to the asset's phrases reaches the asset's gate (the skip rule's fitted gate, `data/labels/assets_gate.json`, section 5g; else `asset.gate_cosine`) or a word pattern of the asset matches it (`inputs/taxonomy/assets_patterns.csv`, optional, the measures' format; always asked). Then the feedback rounds look below the gate (`src/feedback.py`, the measures' method with the asset map's own settings, `asset_feedback.*`): for each asset, the sentences whose own posterior reaches `asset.floor` are averaged, minus `asset_feedback.negative_weight` times those asked that do not; the next `asset_feedback.batch` sentences nearest that query and not yet asked are asked; an asset stops when a round finds fewer than `asset_feedback.min_yield`, or after `asset_feedback.max_rounds` (0 turns the rounds off: an option to compare, not a settled choice). One read of the embedding matrix serves every asset in a round. `route` (embedding, pattern, both, feedback) and `round` say why each question was asked. A row not asked is kept with `asked` false. A gate of 0 asks every asset of every sentence (no rounds). The skip rule treats a pattern-matched yes as always asked, as for measures.

**Which assets a sentence has** (`measure.sentence_assets`, applied where the map is read: the measure stage, and `wbg.unit_assets` at every publish). Each score goes through the asset's probability layer when one is fitted (section 5g). The sentence's own assets count when their posterior reaches `asset.floor` (level `unit`). Otherwise, for each asset, its own score and every earlier sentence's in the paragraph are added up, the k-th sentence back weighted by `asset.decay` to the power k, and the assets whose sum reaches `asset.paragraph_floor` take the sentence (level `paragraph`). With `asset.multiple` (on) every such asset counts, best first; without it, the best only. Else the sentence has none.

**The model** is Jev through OpenRouter's decisions endpoint, pinned to the dated snapshot `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917` (the alias moves when the vendor ships a release). It returns a probability per grade. Answers are stored per model, state and question and re-read on every run (`src/answers.py`), so a changed definition re-asks that asset's question only. Items are batched many to a request (`ask.batch_items`, up to `ask.batch_max_chars`); every answer is still stored under its own item and question. An answer missing a grade is not stored; a run with failed requests writes nothing and resumes on the next run; a refusal (bad key, no credit) stops at once.

**Output.** `data/classify/assets-<version>/scores.csv` (sentence by asset: whether asked, the best cosine, each grade's probability, the model's confidence, the snapshot served, the prior inputs) and a manifest with the exact questions. The version is a SHA-256 over every sentence's first-pass request, the gate, the fitted gates, the patterns and what the rounds read (`asset_feedback.*`, `asset.floor`, the asset layers), so a changed definition, text or gate is a new version, and a version whose answers are all stored costs nothing. Loaded as `wbg.paragraph_grades` (one row per sentence and asset, with `posterior_probability`) and `wbg.unit_assets` (each sentence's asset and level).

**Checking.** Graded asset sheets come later: a new sheet will grade sentences against the assets directly. Until then the asset layers and gates are fitted from the assets people gave sentences on measure sheets (`check_units.fit_asset_layers`).

**Several assets.** With `asset.multiple` (on), a sentence takes every asset whose posterior reaches `asset.floor`, best first; with none of its own, every asset whose decayed paragraph sum reaches `asset.paragraph_floor`. Each asset is its own question, as each measure is. `asset_links` (Postgres `linked_asset_ids`) lists them; `asset_link` is the best. `wbg.unit_assets` has one row per asset (`asset_rank`), and a committed sentence with two assets belongs to both commitments.
