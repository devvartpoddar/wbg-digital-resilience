# The asset map

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5b. The asset map (`./wbg_portfolio run --only classify`)

**Two levels (2026-11).** There are four assets: connectivity, hosting, platforms, and monitoring and control (`assets.csv`, each a one-line list of what it is). Each has types in `assets_subtypes.csv` (for example fibre, mobile and wireless, satellite, international and institutional under connectivity), always ending in `unspecified` for a sentence whose asset is clear but whose type is not stated. Cybersecurity is not an asset. The one rule about what belongs to an asset is said once, at the end of every asset question, not in each definition: "Equipment, training or a policy counts for the asset it is for." (`questions.ASSET_RULE`). Two flags sit beside the asset, defined in `flags.csv`: `digital` (how far the sentence is about a digital technology, graded 0-3) and `policy` (a law, strategy, standard or plan, as against an investment). The flag files are definitions only; they are not label sets that are searched.

**The questions (2026-11-21).** Every asset question is one plain question on the taxonomy's own scale (`inputs/taxonomy/assets_scale.csv`, shared with the digital question): 0 not at all, 1 mentioned in passing or as background, 2 partly or as the means of doing something else, 3 what the sentence is about; grades 2 and 3 count (`read.grades`). The first pass asks, per asset, "How far is the sentence in the 'sentence' field about {asset}? {asset} are: {definition}" and the shared rule. A package is asked the same, "How far is the procurement package in the 'package' field for {asset}?", on the same scale with "package" for "sentence".

The asset set is `inputs/taxonomy/assets.csv` with its phrases (`assets_queries.csv`) and word patterns (`assets_patterns.csv`); it changes rarely and deliberately, because a changed definition is a new question and every grade given under the old wording is a grade of another question. Every sentence the search reads is asked, for each active asset, its graded question, through the same funnel as a measure: the gate (the fitted gate, else `asset.gate_cosine`), the patterns, every sentence already answered, then the rounds counted at `asset.ask_floor` with `asset_feedback.max_rounds` (0 turns them off; the other round settings are the measures'). One read of the embedding matrix serves every asset in a round. The scores are rows of `wbg.scores` with kind `asset`.

**Which assets a sentence has** is read at publish (`decide.sentence_assets`; in no run key). The sentence's own assets count when their posterior reaches `asset.floor` (level `unit`). Otherwise, with `asset.decay` above 0, each asset's own score and every earlier sentence's in the paragraph are added up, the k-th sentence back weighted by `asset.decay` to the power k, and the assets whose sum reaches `asset.paragraph_floor` take the sentence (level `paragraph`). With `asset.multiple` every such asset counts, best first; without it, the best only. Else the sentence has none, and a commitment on it shows `(no asset)` on the dashboards.

**Second look and types.** The first pass gives a sentence an asset only when the decision model, shown the sentence alone, is sure. Two chat-model steps (the pinned `second.model`, one item a request, temperature 0) finish the job for the sentences that count: those committed to a measure by the band rule and digital by the digital question (`decide.committed_digital_units`). Both run on the measure stage after the digital question.

- **`asset_second`** (`second.asset_second`): for a kept sentence whose best first-pass asset is under `asset.ask_high` (1.01: every kept sentence; the first pass still wins at `asset.floor` and the chat model's asset fills only where there is none; where both exist the chat model's is published in `unit_assets.second_look_asset_id` so agreement can be measured), the chat model is shown the sentence with its lead-in, the section, the component and the paragraph, and asked one thing: "Which digital asset is the sentence in the 'sentence' field about? Pick one, or none. Pick a second only if the sentence itself names two.", then the four assets ("id: name: definition") and the shared rule. The type is not asked here. Stored in `wbg.scores` as kind `asset_second` (label set `assets`, label `second`); the answer's `labels` are asset ids (an answer stored before 2026-11-21 held `asset/type` pairs; only the asset is read).
- **`asset_subtype`** (`second.asset_subtype`): for every asset that counts for a kept sentence, whichever look found it (so a second-look asset gets its type too; it runs after `asset_second`), the chat model is asked "Which type of {asset} is the sentence in the 'sentence' field about?" with the asset's types by name and "unspecified: not stated". Kind `asset_subtype`, one row per sentence and asset (label = the asset).
- **Nothing invented is stored.** A reply naming an asset not in `assets.csv`, the same asset twice, more than two, or a type that is not one of the asset's, is an unreadable reply: counted, stored as a failure, asked again, never read as an answer. A request that failed leaves the sentence with no second look, which is never a yes.
- **Types by name.** `wbg.asset_subtypes` publishes every type's name and definition (and `wbg.flags` the digital and policy definitions); `asset_map` shows `subtype_name` beside `subtype_id`. The type question shows names only; the definitions in `assets_subtypes.csv` are not shown to the model.
- **In the tables.** `unit_assets` rows carry `asset_id`, `subtype_id` and `asset_level`: `unit` (first pass, with its probability), `second_look` (the chat model; no probability), `paragraph` (the decayed sum, off while `asset.decay` is 0) or `none`; up to two assets a sentence. Every level but `none` counts for a commitment and its links. Both steps are the model's reading, not a person's; the types are not yet checked against graded sentences.

Loaded as `wbg.paragraph_grades` (one row per sentence and asset asked, with `posterior_probability`) and `wbg.unit_assets` (each sentence's assets and level, one row per asset).

**Checking.** The combined draw grades asset lines (0-3) on the sentences it shows, and the assets of a shown commitment come with its measure line; the evaluation fits the asset layers and gates from them (section 5g). Issue #75 records that the model, reading the sentence alone, misses most assets a person reading the paragraph gives; `ask.context` = `paragraph` is the test for that.
