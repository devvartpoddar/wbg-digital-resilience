# The asset map

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5b. The asset map (`./wbg_portfolio run --only classify`)

**Two levels (2026-11).** There are four assets: connectivity, hosting, platforms, and monitoring and control (`assets.csv`). Each has finer kinds in `assets_subtypes.csv` (for example fibre, mobile and wireless, satellite, international and institutional under connectivity), always ending in `unspecified` for a sentence whose asset is clear but whose kind is not stated. Cybersecurity is not an asset: a security sentence takes the asset it protects, and national or sector-wide security policy has none. Equipment, training and policy take the asset of the system they are for, and none when no system is named. Two separate yes-or-no flags sit beside the asset, defined in `flags.csv`: `digital` (is the sentence about something digital) and `policy` (a law, strategy, standard or plan, as against an investment). The flag files are definitions only; they are not label sets that are searched.

The asset set is `inputs/taxonomy/assets.csv` with its phrases (`assets_queries.csv`) and word patterns (`assets_patterns.csv`); it changes rarely and deliberately, because a changed definition is a new question and every grade given under the old wording is a grade of another question. Every sentence the search reads is asked, for each active asset, the graded question of section 5g on the shared scale, through the same funnel as a measure: the gate (the fitted gate, else `asset.gate_cosine`), the patterns, every sentence already answered, then the rounds counted at `asset.ask_floor` with `asset_feedback.max_rounds` (0 turns them off; the other round settings are the measures'). One read of the embedding matrix serves every asset in a round. The scores are rows of `wbg.scores` with kind `asset`.

**Which assets a sentence has** is read at publish (`decide.sentence_assets`; in no run key). The sentence's own assets count when their posterior reaches `asset.floor` (level `unit`). Otherwise, with `asset.decay` above 0, each asset's own score and every earlier sentence's in the paragraph are added up, the k-th sentence back weighted by `asset.decay` to the power k, and the assets whose sum reaches `asset.paragraph_floor` take the sentence (level `paragraph`). With `asset.multiple` every such asset counts, best first; without it, the best only. Else the sentence has none, and a commitment on it shows `(no asset)` on the dashboards (`dashboard.commitment_link_levels`).

**Second look and kinds (2026-11-20).** The first pass gives a sentence an asset only when the decision model, shown the sentence alone, is sure. Two chat-model steps (the pinned `second.model`, one item a request, temperature 0) finish the job for the sentences that count: those committed to a measure by the band rule and digital by the digital question (`decide.committed_digital_units`). Both run on the measure stage after the digital question, and ask nothing while `band.on` is false.

- **`asset_second`** (`second.asset_second`): for a committed, digital sentence whose best first-pass asset is under `asset.ask_high` (1.01: every committed, digital sentence; the first pass still wins at `asset.floor` and the chat model's asset fills only where there is none; where both exist the chat model's is published in `unit_assets.second_look_asset_id` so agreement can be measured), the chat model is shown the sentence with its lead-in, the section, the component and the paragraph, and the four assets with their definitions and kinds. It names up to two assets (`asset.multiple`) or none, and one kind for each. Stored in `wbg.scores` as kind `asset_second` (label set `assets`, label `second`); the answer's `labels` are `asset/subtype` pairs.
- **`asset_subtype`** (`second.asset_subtype`): for a sentence that has a first-pass asset, the chat model picks only the asset's kind, from that asset's own list. Kind `asset_subtype`, one row per sentence and asset (label = the asset).
- **Nothing invented is stored as valid.** An asset id that is not in `assets.csv`, a second mention of one, or a kind that is not in `assets_subtypes.csv` for its asset is discarded and counted in the run's log; an asset kept with an unknown kind has no kind (`subtype_id` empty). A kind picked by `asset_subtype` that is not one of the asset's is an unreadable reply: counted, stored as a failure, asked again, never read as an answer. A request that failed leaves the sentence with no second look, which is never a yes. More than two assets, or a reply that is not the JSON asked for, is unreadable too.
- **Kinds by name.** `wbg.asset_subtypes` publishes every kind's name and definition (and `wbg.flags` the digital and policy definitions); `asset_map` shows `subtype_name` beside `subtype_id`.
- **In the tables.** `unit_assets` rows carry `asset_id`, `subtype_id` and `asset_level`: `unit` (first pass, with its probability), `second_look` (the chat model; no probability), `paragraph` (the decayed sum, off while `asset.decay` is 0) or `none`; up to two assets a sentence. `dashboard.commitment_link_levels` (now `unit|second_look|none`) says which levels count on the dashboards. Both steps are the model's reading, not a person's; the kinds are not yet checked against graded sentences.

Loaded as `wbg.paragraph_grades` (one row per sentence and asset asked, with `posterior_probability`) and `wbg.unit_assets` (each sentence's assets and level, one row per asset).

**Checking.** The combined draw grades asset lines (0-3) on the sentences it shows, and the assets of a shown commitment come with its measure line; the evaluation fits the asset layers and gates from them (section 5g). Issue #75 records that the model, reading the sentence alone, misses most assets a person reading the paragraph gives; `ask.context` = `paragraph` is the test for that.
