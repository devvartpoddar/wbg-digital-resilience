# The asset map

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5b. Classification: the asset map (`src/classify.py`, `./wbg classify <set>`)

**A sentence map (2026-10-22).** The map now grades each sentence the search reads (the paragraph map described below, and the switch that kept it, were removed the same day: every question is about a sentence), with its section and component, asked the same graded question as the measures (`classify.graded_question`), and read as the probability of grades 2 and 3 through a probability layer per asset when one is fitted (`data/labels/assets_posterior.json`, from the assets people gave graded sentences; src/posterior.py). The cosine gate (`asset.gate_cosine`, 0.25) applies to the sentence's own cosine. A sentence has an asset when its best reaches `asset.floor` (0.3); a sentence that names none takes the last asset named earlier in its paragraph, its probability multiplied by `asset.decay` (0.4) per sentence back (on 615 graded sentences, 0.4 and 0.3 were best; it moved agreement with the person's asset from 0.64 to 0.66, and with a digital asset from 0.58 to 0.62, in sample). The component level is gone. The table keeps its name, `paragraph_grades`, with a `unit_id` column, so the dashboards' questions keep working. Assets and measures are asked the same way on purpose (Dev, 2026-10-22): grades 0 to 3, grades 2 (supported) and 3 (financed) counted. One choice question per sentence (which asset, or none) was tried the same day: it agreed with Dev's asset as often (0.66 against 0.65) at 42 per cent of the cost, but it gives no grade, so financed and supported could not be told apart, and it was not taken. Every row also carries the prior inputs (`prior_*`, section 5g): the cosine and the phrase match weighted by word rarity; the paragraph counts are made after every stage (`wbg.unit_paragraph_context`); nothing reads them until a graded sheet fits the weights and cut-offs that use them. The sentence map asks every asset again: at the test rate, with the short per-item questions (section 5c), about US$2 to 3 for a full run against the paragraph map's US$1.0 to 1.2 (a dry run gives the figure).

Search finds where an asset or a measure is talked about; classification reads the paragraph and says how. Every narrative and annex paragraph given to the model, every footnote given to the model and every table row the search reads as prose (18,834 on the current run) is sent once, with one graded question per active label in the set. The grades are kept by people in `inputs/taxonomy/<set>_scale.csv`:

- assets: 0 not about it; 1 mentioned only as background or another party's work; 2 supported without paying for the asset (technical assistance, policy, studies); 3 financed (built, bought, installed, upgraded, connected or operated);
- a measure's scale lives with its set in `analysis/labels/<set>_scale.csv` and is asked by the measure stage (section 5c), not here.

The state sent with each paragraph is its section title, its sub-component (or component) number and name (from `components.csv`, or the heading's own title), and its text. The model is TypeSafe's Jev through OpenRouter's decisions endpoint with the project's key, pinned to the dated snapshot `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`, which the endpoint accepts (the alias `jev-1.13` moves when the vendor ships a release; 1.14 is live). Until 2026-10-20 the asset map asked under the alias; its stored answers are reused for the snapshot wherever the snapshot that served them was this one (`classify.ALIASES`, `answers.ask(aliases=...)`), so pinning re-asked nothing. It returns a probability for each grade, not text. Its probabilities move a hundredth or two between calls, so every answer is stored once, per model, state and question (`src/answers.py`, `data/classify/answers/`), and every later run reads the store; the stored answers are the record. Storing per question rather than per request is what keeps tuning cheap: a request's tokens are mostly its questions (nine asset definitions with criteria are about 2,600 tokens against 180 for a paragraph), so a changed definition re-asks one question per paragraph, about US$0.35 on this corpus, not all nine (about US$1.90). An answer missing any grade is not stored, and a run with failed requests writes nothing and resumes on the next run; a refusal (a bad key, no credit) stops the run at once rather than sending the rest. Output: `data/classify/<set>-<version>/scores.csv` (paragraph by label: whether the question was asked, the probability of each grade, the most likely grade, the expected grade, the model's confidence, the snapshot served) and a manifest holding the exact questions; the version is a SHA-256 over each paragraph id, the exact request planned for it (model, state and the questions asked) and the cosine gate, so a changed definition, grade, component name, text or gate is a new version; a version already written is found before anything is asked, and a new version whose answers are all stored costs nothing. A label set with no active label is refused, here and by the search. Cost on the current corpus: US$1.88 for the nine-asset map over 18,834 paragraphs (12 minutes at six parallel requests).

**The cosine gate (`--min-cosine`).** At scale a label's question can be put to a paragraph only when a unit of it scores at least the threshold against the label's phrases in the search; the rows not asked are kept with `asked` false. Measured against the full map on the 70-project corpus: 0.25 asks 58 per cent of the questions and loses 5.8 per cent of the links at probability 0.5 (4.0 per cent at 0.9); 0.30 asks 39 per cent and loses 9.0 (5.4). The loss falls on paragraphs where no sentence resembles any phrase for that asset. On by default at 0.25 since 2026-10-21 (`classify.MIN_COSINE`, Dev's decision): about 42 per cent of the asset map's questions are not asked, at the measured cost of 5.8 per cent of the links at 0.5. `--min-cosine 0` asks every question: the full map is the reference any gate is measured against. Moving to the gate costs nothing on the box: every gated question is one the full map already stored.

**Checking.** The asset map is checked the way a measure is (section 5c, `./wbg check`): a drawn sheet, grades keyed on the text and the exact question, precision and recall with intervals. The earlier paragraph check sheet and its evaluator were retired on 2026-10-20 as unreachable from the command line.

**Early warning v5 and access v5 (2026-10-21, from Dev's graded sheet).** Disease and outbreak surveillance, and advisory services for farmers or other users, are digital systems even when called an early warning system (7 of the sheet's disagreements). Access and connectivity includes the power supply and climate protection of the connected sites, user centres and public access points, and the measure stage's "not a digital asset" option says power, cooling or protection for a digital site concerns that site's asset (5 more). Kept as they were, against the grader's suggestion: disaster recovery's backups sit with data hosting (its definition names backup sites and continuity), social registries and land information systems are digital systems, and a telecom-sector regulation takes the network it governs, since access and connectivity is the last mile only. Both edits re-ask those two assets' questions on every paragraph; a definition is also a search query, so the run starts at the search.

**Digital systems, v3 (2026-10-21).** Named systems only: a sector or government system or application the text names; end-user equipment counts only when bought for a named system; generic IT equipment, hardware or software is not this asset (Dev). It had become the default answer for any IT wording. The change re-asks the asset map's "digital systems" questions only; the other twelve assets keep their stored answers. Operational technology and institutional networks already said what they do not include (hazard sensors are early warning; the last-mile link to a named school is access): their overlap with those assets came from the yes/no question format, which the measure stage replaced with one choice (section 5c).

**The asset map.** The assets classification is run once per corpus and read by every measure (section 5c): for each paragraph, the asset with the highest probability of grade 2 or more (supported or financed), and for each component the best of its paragraphs. It is the digital-asset link a measure's question deliberately leaves out.
