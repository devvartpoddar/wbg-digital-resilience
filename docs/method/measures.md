# Measures and their types

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

**Versions.** Every file in `analysis/labels/` carries a `version` column for people (v2.0 since the taxonomy rewrite of 2026-10-22). Runs and stored answers are keyed on the exact questions, never on it.

## 5c. A measure through the funnel

Each measure is one label set in `analysis/labels/`: a definition that names the intervention and never the asset (the model reads literally: a tower-siting sentence scored 0.17 for "a climate risk assessment of its digital infrastructure" and 0.97 for "a climate risk assessment"), search phrases, optional word patterns and a type list. The scale is shared (`analysis/labels/scale.csv`). Every number is a control-panel setting.

1. **The pool and the rounds** are the funnel of section 5g (`src/wbg_portfolio/funnel.py`): word patterns, the gate on the best cosine over the measure's phrases and definition, every sentence already answered, then the feedback rounds counted at `floor.ask`.
2. **The decision model** reads each sentence once, with its list lead-in, section and component (`ask.context` adds the paragraph). Grades 2 and 3 count together.
3. **The decision** is read at publish (`src/wbg_portfolio/decide.py`): the score through the measure's layer when fitted, then the band rule of section 5g (committed at `band.keep`, or in the band when the context score and the chat model's grade agree), as `passes_measure_rule`; `is_committed` is that and digital (kept); the asset from the asset map (section 5b); the types at `floor.types`. Every row carries the wording's firmness (`activity`, `outcome`, `none`, from fixed word lists) and its template wording (`template_projects`, `template_share`): columns to filter on, never cuts.

**Policy is filed under its measure (2026-11).** Whether a sentence is a policy instrument is its own flag (`policy`, `inputs/taxonomy/flags.csv`), so measures no longer carry "policy" types of their own: a law, standard or plan about one measure is filed under that measure, and `climate_governance` keeps only a climate policy, regulation or standard for the digital sector as a whole (definition of 2026-11-21: "A climate policy, regulation or standard for the digital sector as a whole, on resilience, adaptation or emissions. Not a policy about one measure alone, which is that measure."). Whether a sentence is about something digital is a second flag (`digital`), which is why definitions never name the asset. Cybersecurity, e-waste and early warning have no policy type of their own.

**Adaptation applications** (applications that turn climate or weather information into advice or decisions) are part of `climate_information_systems`, as its type "Adaptation advice and decision support".

**Families.** Every measure names its family in its file's `family` column (`climate`, `cloud_data`), or several joined by `|` when it belongs to more than one (disaster recovery is `climate|cloud_data`). A measure is searched, asked and stored once whatever its families, so belonging to two asks nothing twice and counts no sentence twice; the published `family` column carries the value as written, and a reader that shows one family at a time must test membership, not equality. Families share the documents, the asset map, the grade scale and the control panel and nothing else: a measure is searched, graded, typed and linked on its own, and `wbg.labels`, `wbg.commitments` and `wbg.measure_summary` carry the family so the app shows one at a time. Adding, editing or switching off a measure therefore asks nothing about another family's measures and moves none of its commitments. The probability layer, when on, pools the labels of a decision kind across families (`posterior.pool`): a fit, not a question, but it is the one place a family's grades could move another's figures.

**Switching a measure off.** A set whose labels are all inactive is not run and its rows leave Postgres at the next publish; its stored answers stay in the store, so switching it back on asks nothing. A measure, type or phrase that is dropped for good is deleted from the files: git keeps it.

## 5d. A measure's types

1. **Propose**, only for a measure with no list a person wrote: the committed sentences go to a generative model (`checks.GEN_MODEL`) once, and the list is written to `data/types/<set>_types.csv` for a person to approve into `analysis/labels/<set>_types.csv`.
2. **Assign**: every kept unit of the measure is asked one fit question per approved type (yes, unsure or no), told the sentence is already a commitment to the measure, so it asks only which type (its stored wording says "kind" and is left as it is so its answers stand). At publish every type at `floor.types` counts, best first (`types.multiple`; the best only when off); a sentence no approved type reaches takes the OTH types it was asked (those proposed by the gap step, `types.gaps`); a sentence no type reaches has none, shown as `(no type)`. Policy is a flag on a sentence, not a type: a policy sentence often has no type, and what it is is carried by the instrument flag. A measure type is also a key of the commitment rule (section 5g, "Commitments"): a policy sentence, or an investment sentence with no asset, is grouped by its types.
3. **Merge and rename** are file edits, never a model call: `merge_into` folds a type into another of the same measure, `display_name` changes what is shown while `type_name` stays as asked; `./wbg_portfolio publish` applies them.
