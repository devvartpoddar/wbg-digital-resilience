# Measures and their types

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5c. A measure through the funnel (`src/measure.py`, `./wbg measure <set>`)

At 70 projects the decision model could read every sentence for every measure (about US$0.80 a measure); at the 500 projects the study is heading for, it could not for every measure. So a measure is read through a funnel, each level cheaper than the next and each one's loss measurable:

1. **The pool** (section 5a): the units a measure's word patterns match, plus every unit whose best cosine over the measure's phrases reaches 0.45 (`--min-cosine`; `--top N` by count is off unless asked for). On climate risk assessment, top 2,000 was a cosine of 0.473 and 0.45 kept 168 of 171 committed units from a pool of 3,070; a threshold carries between a rare measure and a common one where a count does not. No model reads anything here.
2. **The asset map** (section 5b), as two columns on every unit: the best asset in its paragraph with its probability of grade 2 or more (`asset_para`, `asset_p_para`), and the best in its component (`asset_comp`, `asset_p_comp`). The map covers narrative and annex prose, footnotes and prose table rows; the climate annex tables are where most commitments sit, and a map that skipped them (as the first run did) left 40 per cent of the committed units with no asset score at all.
3. **The decision model** reads each unit in two passes. The first carries the measure's graded question about the sentence (with its list lead-in; `--context paragraph` adds the paragraph, which made the model grade the paragraph rather than the sentence and doubled the false positives on the hand-labelled activities, so the sentence alone is the default). The second, only for units graded 2 or more (about 8 per cent), carries one choice question for the asset (which one digital asset the sentence, read with its paragraph, concerns, with two more answers: "no asset named" and "not a digital asset") and one yes/no question per activity kind (`inputs/config/activity_kinds.csv`: governing, building, sustaining, using: how the commitment is delivered), with the paragraph added to the state (`measure.EXTRAS_CONTEXT`): a climate-annex sentence says "the sites" or "the infrastructure", and the paragraph says which asset that is. On the first graded sheet the sentence-alone asset answer agreed with the grader, who had read the paragraph, on 13 of 28 committed units. The questions are most of a request's tokens, so asking them of the 8 per cent that matter cuts a measure's cost by about four fifths. The asset is therefore answered for the sentence itself: `asset_unit`, `asset_p_unit` and `assets_yes` (the chosen asset), and the kind as `kinds_yes`, `kind_best`, `kind_p`. Until 2026-10-21 the asset was one yes/no question per asset: the model said yes to two or more assets for 70 per cent of committed units, and "digital systems" won most of them, because generic IT wording fits it; asked as one choice, on 167 committed units of six projects, "digital systems" fell from 50 to 4 and 49 chose "no asset named". The combined link (`asset_link`, `asset_link_level`, `asset_link_p`) is the chosen asset when it reaches 0.5 (the sentence names it), else the paragraph map at 0.5 ("a site-specific assessment for individual rooms" in a data-centre paragraph), else the component map, else none. A unit that chose "not a digital asset" takes no link from its paragraph (`asset_link_level` `not_digital`): 23,000 streetlights in a paragraph about towers are not towers. On climate risk assessment 94 of 133 committed units linked at the unit, 72 at the paragraph, 5 at the component and 11 not at all. The measure's question **never names the asset**: the model reads literally, and on a tower-siting sentence the same question scored 0.17 for "a climate risk assessment of its digital infrastructure" and 0.97 for "a climate risk assessment". The model is pinned to the dated snapshot (`typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`), which the endpoint accepts; the alias `jev-1.13` moves when the vendor ships a release. Responses are stored as in section 5a, so a re-run asks nothing.
4. **Relevance feedback.** The first run showed the embedding top 1,000 was not deep enough: committed units kept appearing at the bottom of the ranks (5 in ranks 801 to 1,000). So after round 0 the query is the centroid of the committed units' vectors minus half the centroid of the units graded 0 (Rocchio; the raw centroid pulled in the measure's neighbourhood, "resilient infrastructure" in general), the corpus is re-ranked by cosine to it, the next `--batch` (500) unseen units are graded in one parallel batch, and this repeats until a round yields fewer committed units than `--min-yield` (10) or `--max-rounds` (6) is reached. Each round is one batch of parallel requests, tens of seconds. This is relevance feedback, the one active-learning technique the technology-assisted review literature endorses without caveat; the stopping rule is a yield rule for retrieval depth, not a recall guarantee, and what the search never reaches stays unmeasured until a person grades a sample.

Nothing is gated by default: every candidate and every feedback unit is graded, and the report tabulates how many committed units a gate at 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 or 0.9 on the combined link, the unit answer, the paragraph map or the component map would keep, so the gate's cost in missed commitments is measured before a threshold is chosen. `--gate link|unit|paragraph|component` with `--asset-min` is the setting at scale, once chosen (the unit and link gates apply from round 1 on, since round 0 is what produces the unit answers). On the hand-labelled activities a gate on the combined link at 0.5 cost nothing (19 of 19 kept).

**Two more questions on the same units (`inputs/config/commitment_facets.csv`).** The second pass also asks, of each unit graded 2 or more, whether the commitment is *conditional* (depends on a later study, decision or financing, or is an option) and *who carries it out*, one yes/no per actor: the Bank, the borrower, an operator or utility, a contractor or bidder (`conditional_p`; `actors_yes`, `actor_best`, `actor_p`). They ride with the asset and kind questions, so they cost a few cents a measure. On climate risk assessment, definition v2 (140 committed units): borrower 131, contractor or bidder 114, operator 29, none 2; conditional at 0.5: 17. The pass cost under one US cent.

**Template wording (`units.template_scores`).** Every search unit carries two columns from the corpus alone: `template_projects`, how many other projects print a unit with the same words (lead-in included; case and punctuation aside), and `template_share`, the share of its six-word runs that appear in at least two other projects. The accounting literature on disclosure boilerplate defines boilerplate this way, as word runs shared across many filings. It is computed with flat arrays and 64-bit hashes so memory stays flat at 1,400 projects. They are filters for a reader and features for a later classifier, never a cut: on climate risk assessment the Bank's screening sentence scores high, but so do several real commitments, because the digital projects' climate annexes reuse each other's wording (at a share of 0.5 or more: 10 of 46 screening units and 9 of 136 others), so the score separates weakly here and is not applied to anything.

**A number a person can quote (`src/check_units.py`, `./wbg check <set>`).** The pipeline draws a measure's units by stratum of the model's grade (30 at grade 3, 15 at 2, 20 at 1, 20 at 0, 10 from the feedback round) into a sheet showing the sentence, its paragraph, section, component and the measure's definition, never the model's answer. `--evaluate` stores the grades as labels keyed by the text's SHA-256 and the exact question, and reports precision and recall of "committed" and of "at least planned" against the person's grades, with 95 per cent Wilson intervals on the raw counts and stratum-weighted point estimates. Recall is within the graded pool; what the search never reached is bounded only by the rank-yield curve in the measure report. `--evaluate` also fits a Platt scaling (a logistic fit of the person's grade on the model's log-odds) and stores it in `data/labels/<set>_calibration.json` with the Brier score before and after; the next measure run reads it when its question hash matches the run's exact question (a revised definition starts without a calibration, as it starts without grades; the file is part of the run's version, and the run re-reads the store at no cost) and writes `p_committed_calibrated` on every unit. The fit and the score are on the same rows, so the Brier figure is in-sample until a second sheet exists. The hand-labelled workbook (below) is not a precision figure: its labels are per activity and count plans and the Bank's own screening.

**One workbook for every measure (`./wbg check all --seed <n>`).** Draws every measure with a current run into one workbook, `data/review/check_units_all_s<n>.xlsx`, a tab per measure, by band of the model's probability of the top grade rather than by its grade: 5 below 0.15, 8 from 0.15 to 0.4, 12 from 0.4 to 0.6, 10 from 0.6 to 0.8 and 5 above, so the rows sit where a cut-off would be chosen (`--design bands`; it also works for one set). Each tab has its own key file (`check_units_<set>_s<n>.key.csv`), so `./wbg check <set> --seed <n> --evaluate` reads that tab as it reads a sheet of its own, and `./wbg check all --seed <n> --evaluate` reads every tab. The precision and recall tables are given at P(top grade) of 0.15, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9, so a floor is read off the sheet at no model cost. A tab also takes an optional type per sentence (a dropdown of the set's types and OTH types, defined on the `types` tab), stored with the grade in `data/labels/<set>_units.csv` and reported against the assigned type. The workbook's `package links` tab is the link's sheet (section 5e). A seed already drawn is refused, so a graded workbook is never overwritten.

**What the pool missed (`./wbg check <set> --elusion`).** A random draw of the units the funnel never read, in two strata by their best cosine over the measure's phrases: near (within 0.10 below the pool threshold) and far (the rest), 25 each, graded on the same sheet design; `--elusion --evaluate` weights each stratum's committed count back to its size, with a Wilson interval, and states the pool's implied recall of the corpus given the model's count inside it. This is the elusion test a technology-assisted review is signed off with; the yield rule in the feedback rounds is not one.

Output: `data/measures/<set>-<version>/units.csv`, one row per unit seen (sent or not) with its round, route, search, firmness, template, asset, kind, actor, conditionality and grade columns (and `p_committed_calibrated` once a sheet has been graded) and the unit's text; a manifest with the exact questions, the rounds, the models served, calls, time and cost; and `data/reports/measure_<set>.txt`. The version is a SHA-256 over the search run, the patterns, the asset map's version, the questions, the model, the context design, the gate, the feedback settings and the calibration; the units a feedback round reads depend on the stored answers, which are the record, so an unchanged run reads the store and asks nothing. Loaded to Postgres as `wbg.measure_findings`. On the 70-project corpus a measure costs about US$0.03 once the asset map exists (3,765 units graded, 4 minutes).

**Measuring the funnel (`src/evaluate_activities.py`, `./wbg evaluate <set> --column <theme>`).** A workbook of activities a person labelled by hand (`data/labels/activities.xlsx`, never in git: it holds document text; one row per activity with a project id, a pasted stretch of appraisal text and a flag per theme column) is matched to the current paragraphs by shared eight-word shingles (three or more), and for the activities flagged under a theme the report gives recall at each level: a candidate anywhere in the activity's paragraphs; graded; committed (the top grade); committed and kept by an asset gate at each threshold on the unit answer, the paragraph map and the component map. The rows flagged "Adaptation: No" give the false-positive rate. Labels are per activity, not per sentence, and carry no firmness, so this measures whether the funnel finds a commitment somewhere in the activity, not which sentence or how firm; the match rate is reported first, since an unmatched activity cannot be measured. A disagreement with the workbook is a definitional question for a person, not automatically an error.

**The v4 definitions (2026-10-20).** Dev's revision defines each measure by the intervention, never the asset, with explicit boundaries between neighbours (assessing a risk is climate risk assessment, choosing where to build is siting, changing how it is built is hardening; a second communications path, a second power supply and recovery of data are three measures). Each measure is still one question with no shared preamble, so the rule common to all is carried by the grade scale: grade 1 lists what is context only (a hazard, a lesson, a general aim or claim to be resilient or green without naming what is done, an upstream diagnostic, the World Bank's own screening, Paris Alignment assessment or co-benefit calculation, another agency's work), and grade 3 says a named intervention counts wherever it is printed. That last line was added after a read of the live data: real commitments sit in Paris Alignment and co-benefit paragraphs, often as list items under a lead-in about alignment ("The project is aligned with ... Paris Agreement." followed by "(iv) network redundancy and a backup plan to reroute connectivity"), and a literal reading of the exclusion would drop them. Before the run, the v1 answers showed what v4 is meant to remove: climate governance graded 1,300 units committed, among them Paris Alignment statements and green-design claims; a vulnerability profile was graded committed for adaptation applications; about 262 of 680 renewable-energy units were energy-access programmes (mini-grids, solar home systems), which v4 excludes. Queries, patterns and types that v4 dropped stay in their files with `active` false. Each type list keeps to one axis, what is done: who carries it out is the actor question, so a contractor-required assessment is typed by its scope.

**The v5 definitions (2026-10-21).** Grade 1 also lists a description of systems or practices that already exist, a results-framework indicator or target, an assumption in the economic or financial analysis, and another World Bank project's activities: each was found committed in the v4 answers (accounting backups on compact disks; "Share of alerts…" from a results table; an operating-cost assumption; "other World Bank-financed projects such as…"). The type lists were rewritten to exclude each other (section 5d). Each measure's file carries a `boundary` column, the one-line statement of what the measure is that the cross-measure checks read.

**The v6 definitions and commit floors (2026-10-21).** Dev graded 477 v5 units, about 40 a measure drawn by band of P(grade 3) (`./wbg check all --seed 2`). Every scale's grade 2 now says an activity the sentence itself words as possible or optional ("may include", "could", "would", "where feasible", "as needed") is 2, not 3: with the floors below, 32 of the 89 kept units Dev graded under 3 were worded so, and no floor separates them because they score high. Grade 1 adds benefit claims ("can be used to", "will be leveraged to"), illustrative examples and other donors' activities, and spells out indicators ("number of …", "share of …"). Measure lines from the grader's notes: backup power excludes solar or battery as a site's main supply and hybridised grids (precision was about 0.2 at every floor); adaptation applications must advise users on a specific action, and alerts, surveillance, modelling, land information and master plans belong to neighbours (precision about 0.45); early warning excludes non-hazard alerts and damage reporting; network redundancy excludes transport routes and data replication; burying cable is siting, not hardening; a rule requiring future assessments is governance, not a risk assessment; solar supply is not energy efficiency.

**A commit floor per measure** (`commit_floor` in `analysis/labels/<set>.csv`, read by `labels.commit_floor`): a unit counts as committed at P(grade 3) at or above it, whatever its most likely grade, and where none is written the most likely grade decides (`measure.is_committed`). The run writes the answer into `units.csv` (`committed`, loaded as `measure_findings.is_committed`), and the types, the cross-measure checks, the package links and the views all count those units; asset and kind questions go to them too. Floors read off the v5 sheet (weighted precision / recall, recall over the units the model scored 0.15 or more): climate governance, climate information and siting 0.3 (governance 0.92/0.48 at the most likely grade, 0.95/0.77 at 0.3: real commitments sat below the top grade); risk assessment, disaster recovery, network redundancy and early warning 0.4; energy efficiency, renewable energy and resilient design 0.6 (renewable 0.60/0.80 to 0.84/0.77). Backup power and adaptation applications have none: no floor fixed them. The v6 wording changes every question, so these floors are the starting point; `./wbg check all --design floor` draws about 20 units a measure around each floor (and 30 links around 0.7) to check them.

**Ten items a request, in every stage (`answers.BATCH`, from 2026-10-22).** A request to the decisions endpoint costs a fixed overhead of about 300 tokens plus its state and questions, and the questions are most of it: the measure's question is about 650 tokens against about 50 for a sentence, and the 13 asset questions about 2,600 against about 180 for a paragraph. So the answer store (`src/answers.py`) asks ten items a request in every stage that asks the decision model (the measures, their second pass, the asset map, the types, the cross-measure checks, the package links): each distinct question set out once in the state, each item (a sentence, a paragraph, a pair) in its own field, and a short question per item and question. Every answer is still stored under its own item and question, so a re-run reads it back whatever its neighbours were; stored one-a-request answers stay valid and are never asked again. Measured on 2026-10-21 against one a request, both asked fresh (US$0.17 for every test): the measure question on Dev's 477 graded units cost 74 per cent less with the same accuracy against Dev (precision / recall at the most likely grade 0.77 / 0.80 against 0.77 / 0.83; at P(grade 3) >= 0.5, 0.78 / 0.73 against 0.80 / 0.74); the asset map (60 paragraphs x 13 assets) 61 per cent less, 96 per cent the same grade; the second pass (asset choice, kinds, facets; 60 units) 64 per cent less, 87 per cent the same answer; the type choice (80 units) 58 per cent less, 72 per cent the same type; scope (90) 62 per cent less, 83 per cent the same; the package-link question (60 pairs) 60 per cent less, 80 per cent the same. Nothing but the measure question has a graded sheet yet, so for the others "not the same" says only that the two readings differ, not which is right; the next sheet's type column and link tab measure them. An item's neighbours move its first answer about as much as a small rewording does (on the measure question 86 to 88 per cent the same grade across two neighbourhoods; asked twice in the same batch 96 per cent, as one a request asked twice, 97), which is why a stored answer is never re-asked for its neighbours' sake. A batch may pass the 30,000 characters above which the endpoint reads in its long-context mode (up to `answers.BATCH_MAX_CHARS`, 80,000): the asset map agreed as often at 80,000 as at 30,000 and saved 61 per cent instead of 24. `$WBG_ASK_BATCH=0` asks one item a request.

**Cross-measure checks (`src/commit_checks.py`).** Each measure is graded on its own, so nothing else holds the boundaries between neighbours: in the v4 answers 98 committed climate-information sentences were also committed early warning while 65 others were graded 0 by it, and backup power took in solar supply in general. So once every measure has run, a unit two measures both commit is asked one choice per pair: only the first, only the second, both, or neither, each stated by its boundary. The views drop the unit from a measure the answer says it is not (`dashboard_settings.apply_measure_pair_checks`). On a test of 35 such units, backup power against renewable energy went to renewable energy only in 5 of 7; climate information against early warning to early warning in 3 of 3; siting with climate risk assessment and with hardening came out "both". The same step asks each committed unit its **scope**: a rule on everything of a kind the project buys or builds ("all IT equipment procured will be Energy Star"), one specific investment or activity, or not a commitment. Rules link to every matching package and investments to their best few (section 5e); "not a commitment" is a flag on the commitment, never a cut, because on the test it also caught real lists behind a cross-reference lead-in ("For a complete description … see Annex 5"). Output: `data/checks/checks-<version>/{pairs,scope}.csv`, loaded as `wbg.unit_pair_checks` and `wbg.unit_scope`.

**Switching a measure off.** A set whose labels are all inactive (green data centres since v4: a green data centre is an asset plus other measures) is left out of `labels.label_sets()`: it is not run, and its rows leave Postgres at the next load as a removed set's do. Its stored answers stay in `data/`, so switching it back on asks nothing again.

## 5d. A measure's types (`src/kinds.py`, `./wbg types <set>`)

Clustering the committed units did not recover hand-made types in the earlier trial (adjusted Rand index 0.04 to 0.20), so types are proposed by a generative model and approved by a person:

1. **Propose, only for a measure with no list a person wrote.** The committed units of the current measure run go to the cheapest capable generative model on the project's route (`anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`), which returns named types with one-line definitions and example units, as JSON. The call is made three times with the units in a different seeded order each time (a single call over-splits or merges by chance), every response is stored under the SHA-256 of its request, and the proposals are written side by side (`data/types/<set>-<version>/proposals.md`). Once `analysis/labels/<set>_types.csv` exists (all twelve measures since v4) the list is the person's and this step does nothing; the gap step below finds what the list misses. A comparison of the 2026-10-20 proposals with the v5 lists found about half restated a v5 type, a quarter typed by another dimension (the asset, "solar for telecom sites"; the scope, "tender requirements"; or another measure, "renewable backup" under renewable energy), and the rest were real gaps, added to v5 (protected cable installation, hazard mapping, digitising records, sector early warning, dual links to critical sites, among others).
2. **Assign** (`--assign`; `./wbg add` does it straight after the proposals). A person's `analysis/labels/<set>_types.csv` is the list whenever one is written (else the first proposal run's, `data/types/<set>_types.csv`). Each committed unit goes to the decision model with **one choice question**: which type it is, or none. The v5 lists are written so that types exclude each other, each with what it does not include, and one parent type each was removed or narrowed ("efficient equipment", "warning platform", "deployment configuration", "policy framework"). Asked as one yes/no per type, the model said yes to every plausible type (2.7 types a sentence on climate-informed siting; 44 per cent of project and measure pairs showed three or more types, 77 every type). The choice was once avoided for a first-option bias; on 225 committed units it came out the same in three option orders for every unit, so it is asked in one order. With one type a sentence, a project still shows several types when its sentences do different things, and on a read of the worst cases they mostly did (one Malawi operation: Energy Star equipment, an EDGE-certified data centre, efficient air conditioning, copper replaced by fibre, network standby modes); the wrong ones were wrong measure hits, which the cross-measure checks and grade 1 address. **The gap step**: the units the choice puts in "none" (at least five) go to the generative model with the existing types, which proposes only new kinds the list does not cover, not by asset, scope or another measure and within the measure's definition; each is named with the prefix `OTH.` and assigned at once by a second choice over the OTH types and none. Nothing waits on an approval: the OTH types show on the dashboards beside the approved ones (`data/types/<set>_oth_types.csv`, loaded into `wbg.measure_types`), and a person promotes one by writing it into the list. Output: `data/types/<set>-assign-<version>/assignments.csv` with each type's probability per unit, and `data/reports/types_<set>.txt` with counts by type and projects by type. The load joins the current assignments onto `wbg.measure_findings` (`type_ids_above_threshold`, `best_type_id`, `best_type_probability`) and loads the approved lists as `wbg.measure_types`, so a dashboard filtered to a measure shows its types as the clusters of what the projects commit to.
3. **Merge, rename, regroup: a file edit, never a model call.** Two optional columns in `<set>_types.csv` change what is shown without changing what was asked: `merge_into` names another type of the same measure, and the merged type's yes answers count as the target's (a unit that was "contractor-required" is shown as "site-specific" if the file says so; `wbg.measure_types.merged_from` lists what folded into each type); `display_name` is the name shown, while `type_name` stays the wording the decision model was asked with, so the stored answers keep their key. The loader resolves both (`src/labels.py` `type_resolution`, `src/load_pg.py` `apply_type_resolution`) and the Postgres load is keyed on the resolution, so an edit followed by `./wbg publish` is all it takes. The same holds for the assets: `inputs/taxonomy/assets_groups.csv` puts each asset in a reading group (six on 2026-10-20: digital systems and applications; digital public infrastructure; data infrastructure; networks and connectivity; cybersecurity; early warning and emergency communication systems), loaded as `wbg.label_groups` and joined by the views beside the asset (`linked_asset_group_id`, `best_asset_group_id`, the `asset_group` kind in `measure_finding_lists`). The asset ids and their answers are untouched, so grouping differently is a file edit and a reload, and the graded sheets stay valid. What does cost a call: a new or changed type (the one choice is asked again of every committed unit of that measure, a few cents), and a new measure (section 5c; about US$0.15 per measure on the digital set after the search).
