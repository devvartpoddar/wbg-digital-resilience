# Measures and their types

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

**Versions (from 2026-10-22).** Every file in `analysis/labels/` carries `v1.0` in its `version` column; the earlier numbering (v1 to v7) is retired and appears below only as history. A revision of a set's wording (definition, boundary, scale, types, patterns, queries, floor) raises the minor number of the rows it changes: v1.1, v1.2, and so on. The major number changes only when the decision model that grades the sentences changes (v2.0 for the first set graded by another model). The column is a label for people: runs and stored answers are keyed on the exact questions, never on it, so changing it alone asks nothing again and leaves no run stale.

## 5c. A measure through the funnel (`src/measure.py`, `./wbg measure <set>`)

**One method for every label, and one control panel (2026-10-22).** Every threshold below is now a setting in `inputs/config/control_panel.csv` (read by `src/panel.py`; kept by people, never written by code), with what it does and how to tune it beside it; where a paragraph below gives a number, the panel's value is the one that applies. Every decision is about a sentence, asked in one form of graded question (`classify.graded_question`, word for word the measure question asked since 2026-10-20, so the stored measure answers stay valid), and read the same way: the probability of grades 2 and 3 (`read.grades`; loose first, tightened by the floors). After every graded answer a probability layer (`src/posterior.py`) combines that score with how similar the sentence is, by its embedding, to the sentences a person graded for the same label: a weighted vote of the graded sentences (each weighted by exp(sharpness x (similarity - 1)), `posterior.sharpness` 20) and the similarity to the average graded yes minus the average graded no. A logistic fit on the person's grades (`./wbg check <set> --evaluate`, the only reader of grades) is written to `data/labels/<set>_posterior.json` and applied by the next run as `p_committed_calibrated`; on 742 graded sentences it raised recall at 0.85 precision from 0.78 to 0.82 and at 0.90 from 0.60 to 0.67 (held-out halves; sharpness 10 to 20 best of 2 to 160). A sentence counts as committed (or planned) when that probability reaches its measure's floor (`floor.<set>`, else `floor.default` 0.5); the evaluation report suggests, per measure, the floor whose precision on the graded sentences reaches `floor.target_precision` (0.8). The per-measure `commit_floor` column in `analysis/labels/` is gone: those floors were on P(grade 3) and do not carry over. The asset comes from the sentence asset map (5b), with the last asset named earlier in the paragraph for a sentence that names none; there is no second pass (below). **Every unit also stores its prior inputs** (`prior_*` in `units.csv` and `measure_findings`, section 5g): its cosine to the measure's phrases and the phrase match weighted by word rarity; the paragraph counts (other sentences classified with an asset, or committed for a measure) are made after every stage, in `wbg.unit_paragraph_context`. Nothing reads them until a graded sheet fits the weights and cut-offs that use them.

At 70 projects the decision model could read every sentence for every measure (about US$0.80 a measure); at the 500 projects the study is heading for, it could not for every measure. So a measure is read through a funnel, each level cheaper than the next and each one's loss measurable:

1. **The pool** (section 5a): the units a measure's word patterns match, plus every unit whose best cosine over the measure's phrases reaches 0.45 (`--min-cosine`; `--top N` by count is off unless asked for). On climate risk assessment, top 2,000 was a cosine of 0.473 and 0.45 kept 168 of 171 committed units from a pool of 3,070; a threshold carries between a rare measure and a common one where a count does not. No model reads anything here.
2. **The asset map** (section 5b), as two columns on every unit: the best asset in its paragraph with its probability of grade 2 or more (`asset_para`, `asset_p_para`), and the best in its component (`asset_comp`, `asset_p_comp`). The map covers narrative and annex prose, footnotes and prose table rows; the climate annex tables are where most commitments sit, and a map that skipped them (as the first run did) left 40 per cent of the committed units with no asset score at all.
3. **The decision model** reads each unit once, with the measure's graded question (since 2026-10-22; the second pass this item goes on to describe was removed, see "No second pass" below). Until then it read each unit in two passes. The first carries the measure's graded question about the sentence (with its list lead-in; `--context paragraph` adds the paragraph, which made the model grade the paragraph rather than the sentence and doubled the false positives on the hand-labelled activities, so the sentence alone is the default). A second look, the same question asked again with the paragraph for units scoring 0.3 or more on the sentence, ran from 2026-10-21 (PR #48) and was removed the same day: it had been chosen on Dev's 477 graded units (precision at recall 0.80 from 0.78 to 0.84 there), and on the next, independent sheet (s4, 224 units that had the look, weighted) the sentence-only score ranked commitments better (0.76 against 0.69 on a 0 to 1 ranking measure) and the two were even at the floors (precision / recall 0.66 / 0.83 sentence-only, 0.69 / 0.81 with the look). The second, only for units graded 2 or more (about 8 per cent), carries one choice question for the asset (which one digital asset the sentence, read with its paragraph, concerns, with two more answers: "no asset named" and "not a digital asset") and one yes/no question per activity kind (`inputs/config/activity_kinds.csv`: governing, building, sustaining, using: how the commitment is delivered), with the paragraph added to the state (`measure.EXTRAS_CONTEXT`): a climate-annex sentence says "the sites" or "the infrastructure", and the paragraph says which asset that is. On the first graded sheet the sentence-alone asset answer agreed with the grader, who had read the paragraph, on 13 of 28 committed units. The questions are most of a request's tokens, so asking them of the 8 per cent that matter cuts a measure's cost by about four fifths. The asset is therefore answered for the sentence itself: `asset_unit`, `asset_p_unit` and `assets_yes` (the chosen asset), and the kind as `kinds_yes`, `kind_best`, `kind_p`. Until 2026-10-21 the asset was one yes/no question per asset: the model said yes to two or more assets for 70 per cent of committed units, and "digital systems" won most of them, because generic IT wording fits it; asked as one choice, on 167 committed units of six projects, "digital systems" fell from 50 to 4 and 49 chose "no asset named". The combined link (`asset_link`, `asset_link_level`, `asset_link_p`) is the chosen asset when it reaches 0.5 (the sentence names it), else the paragraph map at 0.5 ("a site-specific assessment for individual rooms" in a data-centre paragraph), else the component map, else none. A unit that chose "not a digital asset" takes no link from its paragraph (`asset_link_level` `not_digital`): 23,000 streetlights in a paragraph about towers are not towers. On climate risk assessment 94 of 133 committed units linked at the unit, 72 at the paragraph, 5 at the component and 11 not at all. The measure's question **never names the asset**: the model reads literally, and on a tower-siting sentence the same question scored 0.17 for "a climate risk assessment of its digital infrastructure" and 0.97 for "a climate risk assessment". The model is pinned to the dated snapshot (`typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`), which the endpoint accepts; the alias `jev-1.13` moves when the vendor ships a release. Responses are stored as in section 5a, so a re-run asks nothing.
4. **Relevance feedback.** The first run showed the embedding top 1,000 was not deep enough: committed units kept appearing at the bottom of the ranks (5 in ranks 801 to 1,000). So after round 0 the query is the centroid of the committed units' vectors minus half the centroid of the units graded 0 (Rocchio; the raw centroid pulled in the measure's neighbourhood, "resilient infrastructure" in general), the corpus is re-ranked by cosine to it, the next `--batch` (500) unseen units are graded in one parallel batch, and this repeats until a round yields fewer committed units than `--min-yield` (10) or `--max-rounds` (6) is reached. Each round is one batch of parallel requests, tens of seconds. This is relevance feedback, the one active-learning technique the technology-assisted review literature endorses without caveat; the stopping rule is a yield rule for retrieval depth, not a recall guarantee, and what the search never reaches stays unmeasured until a person grades a sample.

Nothing is gated on the asset: every candidate and every feedback unit is asked, about the sentence alone. The `--gate` (link, unit, paragraph, component), `--asset-min` and `--context paragraph` options were removed on 2026-10-22, so a measure is asked one way only; what is not asked will be decided by the skip rule of `docs/method/decisions.md` once it is fitted. The report tabulates committed units by the sentence's own asset at 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9 and by asset link level. Earlier evidence: on the hand-labelled activities a gate on the combined link at 0.5 cost nothing (19 of 19 kept).

**No second pass (2026-10-22).** Until 2026-10-22 the units graded 2 or more were also asked whether the commitment is *conditional* and *who carries it out* (the Bank, the borrower, an operator, a contractor), from `inputs/config/commitment_facets.csv`, and before that how it is delivered and which asset it concerns. Nothing decided on those answers, so Dev dropped them; their columns (`conditional_p`, `actors_yes`, `actor_best`, `actor_p`, the kind columns) stay, empty, so the tables and views keep their shape, and `commitments.share_not_conditional` is empty. On the full v7 run the units that had the second pass cost US$1.23 of the measure stage's answers against US$0.65 for the other 57,827 units, though most of that was the asset choice question, which moved to the sentence asset map; the conditional and actor questions alone are estimated at about US$0.1 to 0.25 a full run.

**Template wording (`units.template_scores`).** Every search unit carries two columns from the corpus alone: `template_projects`, how many other projects print a unit with the same words (lead-in included; case and punctuation aside), and `template_share`, the share of its six-word runs that appear in at least two other projects. The accounting literature on disclosure boilerplate defines boilerplate this way, as word runs shared across many filings. It is computed with flat arrays and 64-bit hashes so memory stays flat at 1,400 projects. They are filters for a reader and features for a later classifier, never a cut: on climate risk assessment the Bank's screening sentence scores high, but so do several real commitments, because the digital projects' climate annexes reuse each other's wording (at a share of 0.5 or more: 10 of 46 screening units and 9 of 136 others), so the score separates weakly here and is not applied to anything.

**A number a person can quote (`src/check_units.py`, `./wbg check <set>`).** The pipeline draws a measure's units by stratum of the model's grade (30 at grade 3, 15 at 2, 20 at 1, 20 at 0, 10 from the feedback round) into a sheet showing the sentence, its paragraph, section, component and the measure's definition, never the model's answer. `--evaluate` stores the grades as labels keyed by the text's SHA-256 and the exact question, and reports precision and recall of "committed" and of "at least planned" against the person's grades, with 95 per cent Wilson intervals on the raw counts and stratum-weighted point estimates. Recall is within the graded pool; what the search never reached is bounded only by the rank-yield curve in the measure report. `--evaluate` also fits the probability layer (`src/posterior.py`; `docs/method/decisions.md`) on the same grades. The Platt scaling it also fitted until 2026-10-22 (`data/labels/<set>_calibration.json`) was read by nothing after the layer came in, and was removed. The hand-labelled workbook (below) is not a precision figure: its labels are per activity and count plans and the Bank's own screening.

**One workbook for every measure (`./wbg check all --seed <n>`).** Draws every measure with a current run into one workbook, `data/review/check_units_all_s<n>.xlsx`, a tab per measure, by band of the model's probability of the top grade rather than by its grade: 5 below 0.15, 8 from 0.15 to 0.4, 12 from 0.4 to 0.6, 10 from 0.6 to 0.8 and 5 above, so the rows sit where a cut-off would be chosen (`--design bands`; it also works for one set). Each tab has its own key file (`check_units_<set>_s<n>.key.csv`), so `./wbg check <set> --seed <n> --evaluate` reads that tab as it reads a sheet of its own, and `./wbg check all --seed <n> --evaluate` reads every tab. The precision and recall tables are given at P(top grade) of 0.15, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9, so a floor is read off the sheet at no model cost. A tab also takes an optional type per sentence (a dropdown of the set's types and OTH types, defined on the `types` tab), stored with the grade in `data/labels/<set>_units.csv` and reported against the assigned type. The workbook's `package links` tab is the link's sheet (section 5e). A seed already drawn is refused, so a graded workbook is never overwritten.

**What the pool missed (`./wbg check <set> --elusion`).** A random draw of the units the funnel never read, in two strata by their best cosine over the measure's phrases: near (within 0.10 below the pool threshold) and far (the rest), 25 each, graded on the same sheet design; `--elusion --evaluate` weights each stratum's committed count back to its size, with a Wilson interval, and states the pool's implied recall of the corpus given the model's count inside it. This is the elusion test a technology-assisted review is signed off with; the yield rule in the feedback rounds is not one.

Output: `data/measures/<set>-<version>/units.csv`, one row per unit seen (sent or not) with its round, route, search, firmness, template, asset, kind, actor, conditionality and grade columns (and `p_committed_calibrated` once a sheet has been graded) and the unit's text; a manifest with the exact questions, the rounds, the models served, calls, time and cost; and `data/reports/measure_<set>.txt`. The version is a SHA-256 over the search run, the patterns, the asset map's version, the questions, the model, the context design, the gate, the feedback settings and the calibration; the units a feedback round reads depend on the stored answers, which are the record, so an unchanged run reads the store and asks nothing. Loaded to Postgres as `wbg.measure_findings`. On the 70-project corpus a measure costs about US$0.03 once the asset map exists (3,765 units graded, 4 minutes).

**Measuring the funnel (`src/evaluate_activities.py`, `./wbg evaluate <set> --column <theme>`).** A workbook of activities a person labelled by hand (`data/labels/activities.xlsx`, never in git: it holds document text; one row per activity with a project id, a pasted stretch of appraisal text and a flag per theme column) is matched to the current paragraphs by shared eight-word shingles (three or more), and for the activities flagged under a theme the report gives recall at each level: a candidate anywhere in the activity's paragraphs; graded; committed (the top grade); committed and with an asset at each threshold, on the combined link and on the sentence's own asset. The rows flagged "Adaptation: No" give the false-positive rate. Labels are per activity, not per sentence, and carry no firmness, so this measures whether the funnel finds a commitment somewhere in the activity, not which sentence or how firm; the match rate is reported first, since an unmatched activity cannot be measured. A disagreement with the workbook is a definitional question for a person, not automatically an error.

**The v4 definitions (2026-10-20).** Dev's revision defines each measure by the intervention, never the asset, with explicit boundaries between neighbours (assessing a risk is climate risk assessment, choosing where to build is siting, changing how it is built is hardening; a second communications path, a second power supply and recovery of data are three measures). Each measure is still one question with no shared preamble, so the rule common to all is carried by the grade scale: grade 1 lists what is context only (a hazard, a lesson, a general aim or claim to be resilient or green without naming what is done, an upstream diagnostic, the World Bank's own screening, Paris Alignment assessment or co-benefit calculation, another agency's work), and grade 3 says a named intervention counts wherever it is printed. That last line was added after a read of the live data: real commitments sit in Paris Alignment and co-benefit paragraphs, often as list items under a lead-in about alignment ("The project is aligned with ... Paris Agreement." followed by "(iv) network redundancy and a backup plan to reroute connectivity"), and a literal reading of the exclusion would drop them. Before the run, the v1 answers showed what v4 is meant to remove: climate governance graded 1,300 units committed, among them Paris Alignment statements and green-design claims; a vulnerability profile was graded committed for adaptation applications; about 262 of 680 renewable-energy units were energy-access programmes (mini-grids, solar home systems), which v4 excludes. Queries, patterns and types that v4 dropped stay in their files with `active` false. Each type list keeps to one axis, what is done: who carries it out is the actor question, so a contractor-required assessment is typed by its scope.

**The v5 definitions (2026-10-21).** Grade 1 also lists a description of systems or practices that already exist, a results-framework indicator or target, an assumption in the economic or financial analysis, and another World Bank project's activities: each was found committed in the v4 answers (accounting backups on compact disks; "Share of alerts…" from a results table; an operating-cost assumption; "other World Bank-financed projects such as…"). The type lists were rewritten to exclude each other (section 5d). Each measure's file carries a `boundary` column, the one-line statement of what the measure is that the cross-measure checks read.

**The v6 definitions and commit floors (2026-10-21).** Dev graded 477 v5 units, about 40 a measure drawn by band of P(grade 3) (`./wbg check all --seed 2`). Every scale's grade 2 now says an activity the sentence itself words as possible or optional ("may include", "could", "would", "where feasible", "as needed") is 2, not 3: with the floors below, 32 of the 89 kept units Dev graded under 3 were worded so, and no floor separates them because they score high. Grade 1 adds benefit claims ("can be used to", "will be leveraged to"), illustrative examples and other donors' activities, and spells out indicators ("number of …", "share of …"). Measure lines from the grader's notes: backup power excludes solar or battery as a site's main supply and hybridised grids (precision was about 0.2 at every floor); adaptation applications must advise users on a specific action, and alerts, surveillance, modelling, land information and master plans belong to neighbours (precision about 0.45); early warning excludes non-hazard alerts and damage reporting; network redundancy excludes transport routes and data replication; burying cable is siting, not hardening; a rule requiring future assessments is governance, not a risk assessment; solar supply is not energy efficiency.

**The v7 definitions (2026-10-21).** Dev graded the s4 tuning sheet (331 units, 70 links; `--design tune`) and a review of it proposed 33 changes; those the grades support were made, and those the grades contradict were left out, because a sentence may count under several measures (the cross-measure checks, section 5c, settle pairs). Every scale now says how examples grade (concrete measures listed as what the project will do, "including", "such as", are 3; possibilities, "for example, could", are 2), how technical assistance grades (producing the deliverable, an assessment, a plan or a standard, is 3; training staff to do it later is 1), and adds "will consider", "when applicable", "will seek to" and "to the extent possible" to the hedges of grade 2. Measure lines: adaptation applications turns climate information into a decision or action (advice, prioritising investments or maintenance, selecting who receives support; on s4 its weighted recall was 0.10 because Dev's commitments were of this kind); backup power counts a stated battery reserve whatever charges it, and data-centre tier standards; climate governance must apply beyond this project's assets and be formally adopted, and owns sector continuity rules; climate information counts exposure data kept in a system and excludes emissions accounting; siting is renamed siting, routing and deployment choice, with choosing against strengthening, assessed site selection 3 and "options will be analysed" 2; risk assessment treats an unfinanced "the climate risk assessment" as the Bank's screening and requires hazard content; disaster recovery takes bare "backup" only about data or systems, and recovery-site links; early warning counts warnings before or during a hazard and hazard alerts to road users; network redundancy gives recovery-site links to disaster recovery and sector guidelines to governance, and a stated benefit or an example is not a commitment; renewable energy's scale puts energy access at 0; physical hardening needs a named engineering measure on a named asset (s4 precision 0.40, mostly generic resilience wording and flood works that were the investment). Types: battery backup includes solar-charged batteries with a stated reserve; hazard mapping is a one-off map or study. A fifth delivery kind, requiring in procurement (`inputs/config/activity_kinds.csv`, `require`), makes procurement requirements queryable across every measure (`kinds_yes`). The floors are re-read on the sheets already graded (s2 and s4), chosen on one part and reported on the held-out part, since choosing and reporting on the same units flattered the second look.

**v7 scale lines tightened (2026-10-21, not yet run).** The v7 run (US$1.06) raised committed units from 1,164 to 2,100, most of the rise generic sentences: "The project thus includes support to legal and regulatory environments as a key activity." and "To strengthen data governance, data systems and statistical capacity:" (both graded 0) scored about 0.5, and each stood for 1,200 to 2,700 pool sentences in the lowest band of the s4 draw. The two new grade-3 lines now apply only when the listed item or the deliverable is itself the measure, and grade 1 names headings, lead-ins and general technical assistance or institutional strengthening that do not name it. Tested on Dev's 807 graded units (s2 and s4) before running, as precision at recall 0.90 / 0.80 / 0.70: v6 wording 0.66 / 0.70 / 0.75, v7 0.66 / 0.70 / 0.72, tightened 0.65 / 0.70 / 0.74; with the paragraph 0.65 / 0.71 / 0.75; Gemma 4 31B on the same question 0.70 / 0.74 / 0.78 at about eight times the price. None reaches 0.8 at recall 0.9 on these sheets, which were drawn near the floors and so hold the hard cases; precision on what the dashboards show is higher but cannot be read from them, because their low bands hold only 4 positives among 105 heavily weighted rows. A random sample of committed units would measure it.

**A commit floor per measure, until 2026-10-22** (`commit_floor` in `analysis/labels/<set>.csv`, since replaced by the control panel's `floor.*` on the probability of grades 2 and 3): a unit counts as committed at P(grade 3) at or above it, whatever its most likely grade, and where none is written the most likely grade decides (`measure.is_committed`). The run writes the answer into `units.csv` (`committed`, loaded as `measure_findings.is_committed`), and the types, the cross-measure checks, the package links and the views all count those units; asset and kind questions go to them too. Floors read off the v5 sheet (weighted precision / recall, recall over the units the model scored 0.15 or more): climate governance, climate information and siting 0.3 (governance 0.92/0.48 at the most likely grade, 0.95/0.77 at 0.3: real commitments sat below the top grade); risk assessment, disaster recovery, network redundancy and early warning 0.4; energy efficiency, renewable energy and resilient design 0.6 (renewable 0.60/0.80 to 0.84/0.77). Backup power and adaptation applications have none: no floor fixed them. The v6 wording changes every question, so these floors are the starting point; `./wbg check all --design floor` draws about 20 units a measure around each floor (and 30 links around 0.7) to check them. `--design tune` (2026-10-21) is the same draw at 1.4 times the rows, about 28 a measure, with 70 links over the whole probability range: the sheet that re-sets every floor and the link cut-off (s4, graded 2026-10-21). Re-read on the v6 answers to the same 477 units (same weights, about 35 units a measure), three floors moved where one setting did at least as well on both counts: disaster recovery 0.4 to 0.3 (0.96/0.87 to 0.96/0.96), energy efficiency 0.6 to 0.5 (0.91/0.64 to 0.91/0.87), network redundancy 0.4 to 0.5 (0.81/0.82 to 0.84/0.82). The rest stay: no floor beat them on both, and with about 35 units a measure a trade of one count for the other is noise. Adaptation applications keeps no floor: its recall is under 0.2 at every floor, because units graded 3 under the v5 definition score low under the narrower v6 one, which the next sheet settles.

**Many items a request, in every stage (`answers.BATCH`; ten from 2026-10-22, filled up to `answers.BATCH_MAX_CHARS` from the sentence asset map of the same day: on 615 sentences x 13 assets, 38 requests instead of 56 and about 10 per cent fewer characters, because each item still carries one short question per label, about 270 characters each, which is most of a sentence's cost; on the measure question, filled requests agreed with ten a request on 90 per cent of yes/no calls at 0.5, mean average precision 0.882 against 0.888, about the shift between one and ten a request; on the assets 98.7 per cent). **Each item's short questions carry the grade numbers alone (2026-10-22):** the full question, with every grade written out, is in the request once; the four-word previews were 44 per cent of an asset request's characters and changed nothing measurable (615 sentences x 13 assets: top asset agreeing with Dev 0.65 against 0.66, mean average precision 0.603 against 0.604, US$0.0204 against US$0.0309; measures: mean average precision 0.878 against 0.882, 14 per cent cheaper). A shorter label for each short question saved 5 to 8 per cent more with a small loss on the assets, and dropping section and component from the item saved 3 to 10 per cent more but cost the measures 0.018 of mean average precision: neither was taken.** A request to the decisions endpoint costs a fixed overhead of about 300 tokens plus its state and questions, and the questions are most of it: the measure's question is about 650 tokens against about 50 for a sentence, and the 13 asset questions about 2,600 against about 180 for a paragraph. So the answer store (`src/answers.py`) asks ten items a request in every stage that asks the decision model (the measures, their second pass, the asset map, the types, the cross-measure checks, the package links): each distinct question set out once in the state, each item (a sentence, a paragraph, a pair) in its own field, and a short question per item and question. Every answer is still stored under its own item and question, so a re-run reads it back whatever its neighbours were; stored one-a-request answers stay valid and are never asked again. Measured on 2026-10-21 against one a request, both asked fresh (US$0.17 for every test): the measure question on Dev's 477 graded units cost 74 per cent less with the same accuracy against Dev (precision / recall at the most likely grade 0.77 / 0.80 against 0.77 / 0.83; at P(grade 3) >= 0.5, 0.78 / 0.73 against 0.80 / 0.74); the asset map (60 paragraphs x 13 assets) 61 per cent less, 96 per cent the same grade; the second pass (asset choice, kinds, facets; 60 units) 64 per cent less, 87 per cent the same answer; the type choice (80 units) 58 per cent less, 72 per cent the same type; scope (90) 62 per cent less, 83 per cent the same; the package-link question (60 pairs) 60 per cent less, 80 per cent the same. Nothing but the measure question has a graded sheet yet, so for the others "not the same" says only that the two readings differ, not which is right; the next sheet's type column and link tab measure them. An item's neighbours move its first answer about as much as a small rewording does (on the measure question 86 to 88 per cent the same grade across two neighbourhoods; asked twice in the same batch 96 per cent, as one a request asked twice, 97), which is why a stored answer is never re-asked for its neighbours' sake. A batch may pass the 30,000 characters above which the endpoint reads in its long-context mode (up to `answers.BATCH_MAX_CHARS`, 80,000): the asset map agreed as often at 80,000 as at 30,000 and saved 61 per cent instead of 24. `$WBG_ASK_BATCH=0` asks one item a request.

**Cross-measure checks (`src/commit_checks.py`).** Each measure is graded on its own, so nothing else holds the boundaries between neighbours: in the v4 answers 98 committed climate-information sentences were also committed early warning while 65 others were graded 0 by it, and backup power took in solar supply in general. So once every measure has run, a unit two measures both commit is asked one choice per pair: only the first, only the second, both, or neither, each stated by its boundary. The views drop the unit from a measure the answer says it is not (`dashboard_settings.apply_measure_pair_checks`), when the answer's probability reaches `floor.pair_check` (0 until pair checks are graded: its most likely answer). On a test of 35 such units, backup power against renewable energy went to renewable energy only in 5 of 7; climate information against early warning to early warning in 3 of 3; siting with climate risk assessment and with hardening came out "both". **Is the pair question still worth asking (2026-10-22)?** On Dev's graded sentences with a pair answer (249 of 742; 198 graded yes), recall at 0.90 precision on those sentences was 0.78 with the probability layer alone and 0.87 with the pair answer applied as today (0.73 to 0.75 over all 742; at 0.85 precision 0.83 to 0.85); adding the other measures' scores or the sentence's rank among measures to the layer instead gave nothing (0.78 to 0.81 at best). A softmax across the measures, each measure's probability-layer score (Jev's score, the weighted vote and the centroid difference against that measure's graded sentences), gave nothing either: as an input to the layer 0.78 to 0.79, as a cut with its temperature and cut-off chosen on half the sentences 0.76. The softmax share does sort Dev's grades (0.71 on a 0 to 1 ranking measure on those 249), but the layer already holds what it knows; within thirds of the layer's score the pair answer still sorts them (0.72, 0.68, 0.80) and the softmax share mostly does not (0.56, 0.50, 0.77). The pair question reads the two measures' boundaries; similarity does not. Of the 38 units the answers dropped, 14 were graded yes by Dev, so the question is right about two times in three when it drops. It costs about US$0.02 a full run (1,869 pairs). It stays. Until 2026-10-22 the same step also asked each committed unit its **scope** (a rule, one investment, or not a commitment), and the views linked an investment to its three most likely packages only; that question was dropped with the second pass, so every package at the link threshold now counts, and `wbg.unit_scope` stays, empty (US$0.06 a full run, 3,495 units). Each pair also stores its prior inputs (section 5g): each measure's probability for the unit and its cosine to that measure's phrases. Output: `data/checks/checks-<version>/pairs.csv`, loaded as `wbg.unit_pair_checks`.

**Switching a measure off.** A set whose labels are all inactive (green data centres since v4: a green data centre is an asset plus other measures) is left out of `labels.label_sets()`: it is not run, and its rows leave Postgres at the next load as a removed set's do. Its stored answers stay in `data/`, so switching it back on asks nothing again.

## 5d. A measure's types (`src/kinds.py`, `./wbg types <set>`)

**Prior inputs for the type choice (section 5g).** `assignments.csv` stores, beside each type's probability (`p_<type>`), the type's phrase match against the sentence (`prior_phrase_match_<type>`: its name and definition, words weighted by rarity). Types have no embedded phrases, so there is no phrase cosine. Nothing reads them yet.

Clustering the committed units did not recover hand-made types in the earlier trial (adjusted Rand index 0.04 to 0.20), so types are proposed by a generative model and approved by a person:

1. **Propose, only for a measure with no list a person wrote.** The committed units of the current measure run go to the cheapest capable generative model on the project's route (`anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`), which returns named types with one-line definitions and example units, as JSON. The call is made three times with the units in a different seeded order each time (a single call over-splits or merges by chance), every response is stored under the SHA-256 of its request, and the proposals are written side by side (`data/types/<set>-<version>/proposals.md`). Once `analysis/labels/<set>_types.csv` exists (all twelve measures since v4) the list is the person's and this step does nothing; the gap step below finds what the list misses. A comparison of the 2026-10-20 proposals with the v5 lists found about half restated a v5 type, a quarter typed by another dimension (the asset, "solar for telecom sites"; the scope, "tender requirements"; or another measure, "renewable backup" under renewable energy), and the rest were real gaps, added to v5 (protected cable installation, hazard mapping, digitising records, sector early warning, dual links to critical sites, among others).
2. **Assign** (`--assign`; `./wbg add` does it straight after the proposals). A person's `analysis/labels/<set>_types.csv` is the list whenever one is written (else the first proposal run's, `data/types/<set>_types.csv`). Each committed unit goes to the decision model with **one choice question**: which type it is, or none. It takes its most likely type when that type's probability reaches `floor.types` (0 until types are graded: always its most likely type). The v5 lists are written so that types exclude each other, each with what it does not include, and one parent type each was removed or narrowed ("efficient equipment", "warning platform", "deployment configuration", "policy framework"). Asked as one yes/no per type, the model said yes to every plausible type (2.7 types a sentence on climate-informed siting; 44 per cent of project and measure pairs showed three or more types, 77 every type). The choice was once avoided for a first-option bias; on 225 committed units it came out the same in three option orders for every unit, so it is asked in one order. With one type a sentence, a project still shows several types when its sentences do different things, and on a read of the worst cases they mostly did (one Malawi operation: Energy Star equipment, an EDGE-certified data centre, efficient air conditioning, copper replaced by fibre, network standby modes); the wrong ones were wrong measure hits, which the cross-measure checks and grade 1 address. **The gap step**: the units the choice puts in "none" (at least five) go to the generative model with the existing types, which proposes only new kinds the list does not cover, not by asset, scope or another measure and within the measure's definition; each is named with the prefix `OTH.` and assigned at once by a second choice over the OTH types and none. Nothing waits on an approval: the OTH types show on the dashboards beside the approved ones (`data/types/<set>_oth_types.csv`, loaded into `wbg.measure_types`), and a person promotes one by writing it into the list. Output: `data/types/<set>-assign-<version>/assignments.csv` with each type's probability per unit, and `data/reports/types_<set>.txt` with counts by type and projects by type. The load joins the current assignments onto `wbg.measure_findings` (`type_ids_above_threshold`, `best_type_id`, `best_type_probability`) and loads the approved lists as `wbg.measure_types`, so a dashboard filtered to a measure shows its types as the clusters of what the projects commit to.
3. **Merge and rename: a file edit, never a model call.** Two optional columns in `<set>_types.csv` change what is shown without changing what was asked: `merge_into` names another type of the same measure, and the merged type's yes answers count as the target's (a unit that was "contractor-required" is shown as "site-specific" if the file says so; `wbg.measure_types.merged_from` lists what folded into each type); `display_name` is the name shown, while `type_name` stays the wording the decision model was asked with, so the stored answers keep their key. The loader resolves both (`src/labels.py` `type_resolution`, `src/load_pg.py` `apply_type_resolution`) and the Postgres load is keyed on the resolution, so an edit followed by `./wbg publish` is all it takes. The assets have no grouping: the reading groups added on 2026-10-19 (`inputs/taxonomy/assets_groups.csv`, `wbg.label_groups`) were removed on 2026-10-21 at Dev's request; the views show the asset itself. What does cost a call: a new or changed type (the one choice is asked again of every committed unit of that measure, a few cents), and a new measure (section 5c; about US$0.15 per measure on the digital set after the search).
