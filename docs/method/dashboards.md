# What the app reads

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5f. What the app reads (`sql/views/13_*` to `19_*`)

The Shiny app (`apps.amduat.dev/wbg`, repository `devvartpoddar/applications`, `apps/wbg`) reads views, not tables, through `src/wbg_portfolio/app.py`, and those views are the interface: a column is renamed or removed only with the app changed in step. Every view carries a comment. The views are rebuilt inside the publish's transaction (section 4). Four are materialised, built once per publish and indexed, because as plain views Postgres misjudged their joins: `commitment_units`, `unit_wording_overlap`, `commitment_summary` and `commitment_package_status`. A status that turns on today's date is therefore as of the last publish.

**A commitment** is made by the commitment rule at publish (section 5g, "Commitments"; tables `commitments` and `commitment_members`): one project, one measure, one instrument and one key, `commitment_key` `asset_type` (an investment sentence's asset and its type: `asset_id`, `subtype_id`), `type` (a policy sentence, or an investment one with no asset, by measure type: `type_id`) or `paragraph` (the sentences with nothing else to group them by, a bucket shown as such). Its sentences are kept sentences (`is_committed`: the band rule and digital; section 5g) in an Investment Project Financing operation (`project_info.is_ipf`; `dashboard.ipf_only`); that one filter is applied in `commitment_units`, so the dashboards' commitments (`commitment_summary`) can be fewer than the table's. A sentence with two assets or two types belongs to two commitments, so a sentence count summed across commitments counts it more than once. `commitment_id` is the MD5 of project, measure, instrument and key, the same on every publish. A paragraph in two projects gives its sentences to both. The readings are one row of `wbg.dashboard_settings`, filled from the control panel at publish.

| View or function | One row per | Reads |
|---|---|---|

**Value of a commitment: three candidates, none chosen.** `project_commitment_usd_m` is the project's total commitment from the projects interface, whose two forms disagree on some projects (section 1); `component_cost_usd_m` the cost of the components the sentences sit under, all funding sources, NULL when none is known; `linked_package_estimate_usd` the sum of the linked packages' plan estimates, with `linked_packages_with_estimate` saying how many gave one.

**Package status.** The plan's own status first: Contract completed, Contract signed, Under implementation, Cancelled. Where the plan says pending, a linked award with a signing date makes it Contract signed and a linked notice Tendered; with neither, it is Not in latest plan when the newest plan drops it, else Overdue when its due date has passed and Planned otherwise. `is_upcoming()` is the one place that says which statuses are still to come; `is_cancelled()` (Canceled or Terminated, the list `decide.CANCELLED` that also sets the links' `package_cancelled` flag) the one place that says which are cancelled. `next_date` is kept only while a package is still to come. A completed contract is not delivery.

**The due date** is `due_date(planned, revised)`: the revised date where the plan prints one, else the planned date. The parser keeps a row's last printed date as `revised_date`, which can be a later milestone; `due_date()` is the one place to change the rule.

**A commitment's status** is the first of its packages' statuses in `package_status_order` (Overdue, Planned, Tendered, Under implementation, Contract signed, Contract completed, Not in latest plan, Cancelled), or No package found.

**Same wording across projects.** Each committed unit's text is cut into runs of `text.shingle_words` words; another project counts when its committed units hold at least `dashboard.near_copy_share` of them (programmes print one climate section with small edits).

**Dropped by a restructuring.** True only where a later restructuring paper marks the component of every sentence with a known component "Marked for Deletion"; NULL when no sentence sits under a known component. Absence from a later paper is not read as dropping.

**Asset map agreement.** `asset_in_asset_map` says whether a sentence of the project names the commitment's asset (`project_assets.sentences_naming_it`): a commitment whose asset no sentence names is worth a second look.

## The app

`src/wbg_portfolio/app.py` holds every query the app runs and what it computes beyond the views; the app (`apps/wbg/app.py` in the applications repository) only lays them out. It is read only: no write, no model call, no embedding. It reads one frame of commitments (`app.commitments`, a few thousand rows) and filters it in memory, so every chart, board and drill on a page counts the same rows; the sentences are read only for the rows a drill shows (`app.sentences`, every sentence of a commitment; `app.evidence`, its strongest). A switch counts every chart in commitments or in projects (`app.tally`), and the projects a share is taken of come from `app.projects`.

| View | What it answers |
|---|---|
| Overview | How many commitments, the linked packages still to come soonest first, where (a world map; regional projects listed apart), by measure, by asset, measure by asset, by approval year, and how far linked procurement has gone |
| Explore, one country | Its projects, which project commits to what (measure by project), its assets, where each project stands |
| Explore, one project | What it commits to, measure by measure, with the strongest sentence; the assets its sentences name; its components and their cost; its documents; its linked packages on a timeline |
| Explore, one asset | Its definition, the measures committed for it, measure by region, where each project stands, a world map |
| Explore, one measure | Its definition and precision on the graded sample, its types (definition, commitments, projects), where each project stands, a world map, type by region and type by asset |
| About | The definitions, which projects are counted, the pipeline as one figure with the count each step passes on (`app.about`), the models and the limitations |

Every bar, cell, country and card opens what is behind it in a side panel: the projects, each opening to its sentences. A sentence behind several commitments of a project (two assets, two types) is shown once, with every measure, type and asset it carries, its stage and its page.

`not_digital_findings` holds the sentences that pass the measure rule but that the digital question says are not about a digital technology: they stay in `measure_findings` (`passes_measure_rule` true, `is_digital` false, so `is_committed` false), count nowhere on the dashboards, and are there for a person to look at what the rule leaves out. `commitment_package_links_in_context` carries the link levels (section 5e), in order of level. The redesigned dashboard reads commitments through the functions below ("The reader for the redesigned dashboard"). The activities (a chat model grouping sentences into activities) were removed on 2026-11-21: the owner rejected model-written activities, and the commitment rule replaces them.

**Computed in `app.py`, not in the views:**

- **Stage** (`app.STAGES`, `app.stage`): a commitment's `status` grouped into Completed (Contract completed), Under way (Under implementation, Contract signed), Coming up (Overdue, Planned, Tendered), Package dropped (Not in latest plan, Cancelled: the package, not the commitment) and No package linked. A commitment with several packages takes the first status in `wbg.package_status_order`. Package links are found for only part of the commitments (the share is in `links.md`; same-asset links raised it), so No package linked is common and says nothing about the project.
- **Place**: the country, or the region for a regional project.
- **Counting projects** (`app.tally` with unit `projects`): a project counts once in each group it has a commitment in, however many, so the groups do not add up to the number of projects. Its share is of a base of projects (`app.projects`: the cohort's projects with a document read, of the instruments `dashboard.ipf_only` admits, with or without a commitment). Grouped by something about the project (country, region, approval year) the base is the projects sharing it; grouped by measure, asset, type or stage it is the whole base. Inside one measure or asset the app passes that measure's or asset's projects as the base, so a type's share is of the measure's projects; inside one country, the country's projects.
- **Where each project stands** (`app.board`): one card per project and stage, with its commitments and types; a project with commitments at two stages has two cards.
- **A package's date** (`app.packages`): the next date while it is still to come, else the signing date, else the due date; `date_kind` says which (planned, signed, was due).
- **Coming up** (`app.coming_up`): one row per linked package whose status is Overdue, Planned or Tendered, with its project, the measures of the commitments it is linked to, its date (the next date, else the date it was due) and the plan's estimate, soonest first.
- **The About page's counts** (`app.about`): one statement per group (projects, text, how sentences reached the model, measures, assets, procurement, definitions, grades), all for the operations the dashboards count and their documents (the measures group: `at_threshold`, the sentences that pass the measure rule, then `kept`, those also digital that count, so the figure shows the digital drop); a group whose statement fails is left out and the page still draws. The thresholds it quotes are read from the control panel.

Countries are mapped from `country_code` (ISO 3166 alpha-2); a regional project has none and is counted beside the map, not on it.

## The reader for the redesigned dashboard

`app.py` also holds the functions the redesigned pages read, each one query over the published tables and views (the existing functions and their columns are unchanged). Filters are keyword arguments, a value or a list; a misspelt one raises `TypeError`. Frames carry plain text in place of lists: several ids or names are joined with `"; "`. Only the operations the dashboards count are read (`dashboard.ipf_only`).

| Function | Returns |
|---|---|
| `families(q)` | one row per family and measure (a measure in "a\|b" is under both), with project and commitment counts for the measure and for the family (`measure_commitments`, `family_commitments`) |
| `commitment_detail(q, family=None, **filters)` (was `activities`) | one row per commitment: place, measure, `commitment_label`, `commitment_key`, type tags (and whether one is a proposed `OTH.` type), up to two assets with their asset type (its own asset first), instrument, component, counts of sentences, paragraphs and documents, first document date and procurement summary with `best_link_level`. `asset_column` is the asset when there is one, else `none` (policy is not an asset, but a policy sentence can be about an asset and then carries it; the instrument says which it is) |
| `commitment_sentences_detail(q, commitment_ids=None, family=None, **filters)` (was `activity_sentences`) | the sentences of those commitments (or of the filtered ones) with document kind, date, page, link, section, component and the strength inputs (`score_first`, `score_context`, `second_grade`) |
| `packages_detail(q, family=None, **filters)`, `package(q, package_id)` | one row per linked package with every procurement field, its asset(s) and their types, link strength and best `link_level`, `is_cancelled`, `is_generic`, the measures its strong links name and the commitments it serves (`n_commitments`, `commitment_ids`, `commitment_labels`), best level first; `package` adds its notices, awards and commitments |
| `not_digital(q, project_id=None, measure=None)` | the sentences that pass the measure rule but the digital question leaves out |
| `asset_types(q)`, `type_definitions(q)` | names and definitions of assets, asset types and measure types, for hover and tables |
| `download(q, level, family=None, **filters)`, `download_notes(q, level, family, filters)` | a sheet per level (`project_flags`, `project`, `commitment`, `sentence`, `package`) with fixed columns (`app.DOWNLOAD_COLUMNS`), and the rows of its notes sheet: what each column means, each measure's definition, the data date, the filters, and the three standing statements |

A commitment's instrument and key are the rule's; its assets are its own (the key's) first, then those most of its sentences name, at most two shown; its type tags are every type any of its sentences has. Its procurement summary counts the packages linked to any of its sentences, with the best level among them. Every count, date and link figure of a commitment (sentences, paragraphs, documents, first document date, linked and strong packages, next date) is read from `commitment_summary`, the one summary: no reader computes them again. Its `sentences` are distinct wordings (a sentence printed word for word in several documents counts once); the sentence download lists each copy. Every count called "commitments" (`measure_summary`, `headline`, `families`, the project download) includes the paragraph groups; `measure_summary`, `headline` and the project download also give `keyed_commitments` and `paragraph_groups`, and `app.measures` returns `graded_scope` so a page can say when a precision is the overall figure. A filter `key` (`asset_type`, `type`, `paragraph`) chooses by the key, `commitment_id` by id. `cohort` (a keyword argument of `families`, `commitment_detail`, `commitment_sentences_detail`, `packages_detail`, `not_digital` and `download`, as `cohort` of the older functions: 'digital' or 'digital_contributor', or none for every project) keeps the projects of that `cohort_set`; the demo seed carries both values. A package filter on `type`, `asset_type` or `instrument` is of the commitments the package serves. In `download`, filters choose rows and never columns; `status`, `strength` and `package_id` belong to level `package` only. At `project_flags` a measure column is 1 where the project has a commitment of the measure within the filters. `app.commitments` (the page frame) reads `commitment_summary` and gains `instrument`, `commitment_key`, `commitment_label`, `subtype_id`, `subtype_name`, `best_link_level` and `best_link_rank`; its rows are now the rule's commitments.

`tests/demo_seed.py` publishes an invented dataset (about 60 projects, no real text, with commitments made by the rule and links with levels) into the schema `wbg_demo` through the pipeline's own loader and views, to build and try a page without the real data: `WBG_PG=... python3 tests/demo_seed.py --schema wbg_demo`, then run the app with `WBG_SCHEMA=wbg_demo WBG_STORE=wbg_demo_store`.
