# What the dashboards read

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5f. What the dashboards read (`sql/views/13_*` to `19_*`)

The dashboards (Metabase collection "WBG v2": Overview, Measure, Project, Commitment) read views, not tables, and those views are the interface: columns may be added, but one is renamed or removed only after telling Dev. Every view carries a comment written for a reader. Views a pivot or heatmap reads are flat (plain columns, no arrays), so Metabase's query builder can use them. The views are rebuilt inside the load's transaction (section 4), so a card never sees them half-built. Two of them are materialised — `commitment_units` and `commitments`, the grain every page reads: as plain views Postgres estimated 27 committed units against thousands, chose nested loops and re-ran the asset-map subquery for every row, so a card reading `commitments` ran for over ten minutes on 2026-10-20 and held the views' locks while the load waited behind it. They are built once per load (not once per card), indexed and ANALYZEd; the views that read them stay plain views. A load or a publish waits two minutes for a lock at most, then says who held it and exits non-zero (section 4) instead of hanging.

**A commitment** is one project, one digital asset, one measure and one type (Dev's decision). Only Investment Project Financing operations count (Dev, 2026-10-20: `project_info.is_ipf`, from the projects interface's instrument, or, where its v3 form gives none, a Project Appraisal Document on file). The sentences that make it up are the units whose most likely grade is 3, in such an operation, whose asset is found in the sentence itself or, where the sentence names none, in its paragraph's asset map (Dev: once a measure is identified, take its asset from the sentence or the paragraph; `asset_from` says which). There is no probability floor and no strength cut-off. A sentence counts under its best asset and its best type above 0.5 (`(no type)` when none); `every_asset_and_type` in the settings would count it under each asset and type it is given instead (on the test slice about seven commitments a sentence, so it is off until Dev decides). `components` lists the component numbers its sentences sit under. A unit two measures commit is dropped from a measure its pair check says it is not, and a sentence that chose "not a digital asset" links to no asset (section 5c). Each commitment carries `scope` (rule if any of its sentences is one, else investment) and `flagged_not_commitment` (every sentence read as not a commitment: a flag, never a cut). `commitment_id` is `md5(project_id|label_set|asset_id|type_id)`, the id the dashboards' links pass, so a reload on unchanged data gives the same ids. A paragraph in two projects (a regional document's programme-wide part) gives its sentences to both. The readings are one row in `wbg.dashboard_settings`: the floor (0), the link levels (`unit`, `paragraph`), IPF only (true), every asset and type (false), the conditional line (0.3), the package-link threshold (0.5), the near-copy share (0.5), whether the pair checks drop units (true), and how many packages one investment keeps (3). On the 17-project slice this rule gives 379 commitments, 45 of them with the asset from the paragraph; the sentence-only rule gave 337.

`findings_under()` stays until no card uses it; its `months` filter reads `package_status`.

**Value of a commitment: three candidates, none chosen (2026-10-21).** `project_commitment_usd_m` is the project's total commitment, the same on every row of the project; it comes from the projects interface, whose two forms disagree on 30 of 131 projects (fetch, section 1), so `project_header` also shows `commitment_v2_usd_m` and `commitment_v3_usd_m`. `component_cost_usd_m` is the cost of the components the commitment's sentences sit under, all funding sources together; it is NULL for the 771 of 1,650 commitments whose sentences sit under no known component (most sit in climate annexes) or when a component has no cost, and a whole-project paragraph filed under the last component (often project management) carries that component's cost. `linked_package_estimate_usd` sums the plan estimates of the packages linked at 0.5 (an investment's best three, a rule's all); `linked_packages_with_estimate` says how many of `linked_packages` gave one (on 2026-10-21, 357 of the 990 commitments with an estimate summed only some of their packages). Medians on 2026-10-21: US$38m a component, US$5m of package estimates.

**Package status.** The plan's own status comes first: *Contract completed* (Completed), *Contract signed* (Signed), *Under implementation* (Under Implementation), *Cancelled* (Canceled or Terminated). Where the plan says pending (Pending, Pending Implementation, Under Review, Planned, or no status), the evidence that moves decides: a linked award with a signing date is *Contract signed*, a linked notice *Tendered*; with neither, the package is *Not in latest plan* when the newest plan no longer lists it (557 of the 850 packages that read overdue on 2026-10-20 were such: dropped or replanned, not late), else *Overdue* when its due date has passed and *Planned* otherwise. A date to act on (`next_date`) is kept only while a package is still to come: a future due date for a planned one, a future deadline for a tendered one; signed, completed, cancelled and dropped packages have none. A package with a linked notice or award is never Overdue. Nothing is called complete or delivered: a completed contract is not delivery.

**The due date** is `due_date(planned, revised)` = the revised date where the plan prints one, else the planned date (Dev's rule: on 2026-10-20, 807 of 1,117 pending packages past their planned date had a revised date still ahead). It decides Overdue, Planned, `upcoming_packages` and `next_date` (the earliest future due date or notice deadline). A caution: the parser keeps a row's last printed date as `revised_date` (section 3.1 step 9), which for a pending package can be a later milestone rather than a new tender date; `due_date()` is the one place to change the rule.

**A commitment's status** is the first of its linked packages' statuses in the order Overdue, Planned, Tendered, Under implementation, Contract signed, Contract completed, Not in latest plan, Cancelled, or *No package found*.

**Same wording across projects.** Word-for-word equality flagged 17 of 2,150 commitments, while four operations of one programme (DRC, Malawi, Angola and the regional Eastern and Southern Africa project) print one climate section with small edits. So each committed unit's text is cut into runs of six words (lower case, letters and digits only), and another project counts when its committed units hold at least half of the unit's runs; `same_wording_projects` is the most other projects any of the commitment's sentences is near-copied in. On the 17-project test slice, 67 of the DRC operation's 80 commitments are near-copied in the regional project.

**Dropped by a restructuring.** A commitment is flagged only where a later restructuring paper's component table marks the component its sentences sit under "Marked for Deletion" (matched by name): true when every sentence with a known component is so marked, false when none is, NULL when no sentence sits under a known component (58 per cent of committed units on 2026-10-20). Absence from a later paper is not read as dropping: short papers restate nothing, which made 38 per cent of commitments read as missing. Only 2 component rows in all restructuring tables were marked for deletion on 2026-10-20, so the column is mostly false or NULL; it is exact where it says true.

**Asset map agreement.** `asset_in_asset_map` says whether the project's asset map grades any paragraph as financing the commitment's asset (NULL for `(no asset)`): a commitment whose asset the map never finds as financed is worth a second look.

**Requested measures (later, not now).** `src/measure_requests.py` and `./wbg requests` hold a queue in `wbg_requests.measure_requests` run one at a time under a US$1 cap a request and US$3 a day (`$WBG_SPEND_CAP_USD`, enforced in `src/answers.py`), writing a requested set's files to `data/requested_labels/`. Nothing schedules it: the write-only Metabase connection, its Postgres role and a timer are yggdrasil changes, and where dashboard-made measures live is Dev's decision.
