# What the dashboards read

Part of the method (`docs/methodology.md` is the index). Measured results and trials: `docs/method/history.md`.

## 5f. What the dashboards read (`sql/views/13_*` to `19_*`)

The dashboards (Overview, Measure, Project, Commitment, New measure) read views, not tables, and those views are the interface: columns may be added, but one is renamed or removed only after telling Dev. Every view carries a comment written for a reader. Views a pivot or heatmap reads are flat (plain columns, no arrays, no table function), so Metabase's query builder can use them.

**A commitment** is one project, one digital asset, one measure and one type (Dev's definition): the committed sentences that make it up are the units whose most likely grade is 3, whose P(committed) is at least the floor, and whose asset link is at one of the counted levels, all set in `wbg.dashboard_settings`. The defaults: no floor (DECIDE: grade 3 includes P(committed) as low as 0.35), the sentence's own link only (`linked_asset_level = 'unit'`), "not waiting on a later study" below a conditional probability of 0.3 (DECIDE), a package linked at 0.5. A unit with no linked asset counts under `(no asset)`, one with no type at 0.5 under `(no type)`; both count in the headline numbers unless Dev decides otherwise. `commitment_id` is the first 16 hex characters of the SHA-256 of `project_id|label_set|asset_id|type_id`, so a reload on unchanged data gives the same ids. A paragraph in two projects (a regional document's programme-wide part) gives its sentences to both.

`findings_under()` stays until no card uses it; its `months` filter now reads `package_status` (Overdue, or Planned within the months).

**Package status.** Notices and signed contracts are evidence that moves; a plan's status column goes stale (all 21 "upcoming" packages of P171305 had planned dates already past while a notice for one had been published). So a package's status is, first that applies: *Contract signed* (a linked award with a signing date), *Tendered* (a linked notice), *Overdue* (the plan says Pending, Pending Implementation, Under Review or Planned and the planned date is past), *Planned* (the same, date today or later), *Planned, no date*, then from the plan alone *Signed per plan* (Signed or Completed), *In process per plan* (Under Implementation), *Canceled* (Canceled or Terminated), *Status unknown*. A commitment takes the first of its packages' statuses in the order Overdue, Planned, Planned no date, Tendered, Contract signed, In process per plan, Signed per plan, Canceled, Status unknown (DECIDE: this puts what needs a conversation first), or *No package found*. Its `next_date` is the earliest future planned date or notice deadline among its packages. Nothing is called complete or delivered: a signed contract is not delivery. On the 17-project test slice, 182 of 337 commitments read Overdue: a commitment links to many packages at 0.5 (up to 49), and any one pending package past its date makes it Overdue, so the order and the link threshold are worth a look before the card is shown.

**Requested measures (`src/measure_requests.py`, `./wbg requests`).** The New measure page's form writes a row to `wbg_requests.measure_requests` (a schema of its own, so no load touches it): the set's short id (`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{2,40}$`, not an existing set), the measure's name, definition, phrases (one per line), optional patterns and cohort set. The runner takes the oldest queued row, checks it (a sentence or more of definition, three phrases or more, patterns that compile), writes the set's files to `data/requested_labels/` (a third folder `src/labels.py` reads after `inputs/taxonomy/` and `analysis/labels/`; outside git, and promoted to `analysis/labels/` only by a person copying the files, DECIDE), with the grade wording of `analysis/labels/backup_power_scale.csv`, and runs the search, the funnel, the type proposals and their assignment and the Postgres write, recording `stage` and `spend_usd` as it goes. Spend is capped at US$1 a request and US$3 a day across requests (`REQUEST_CAP_USD`, `DAILY_CAP_USD`), enforced inside the answer store through `$WBG_SPEND_CAP_USD`: a step that would pass the cap asks nothing, and a request that reaches it ends `stopped` with what it stored. A failed request is never retried on its own. The Postgres role allowed to insert into that table and the Metabase connection that uses it are set up on the box (yggdrasil), not here; so is the timer that runs `./wbg requests`.
